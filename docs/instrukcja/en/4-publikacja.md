# Part IV. Finished publication {ikona=pdf}

Preview, preflight check, PDF file and preview link.

# 19. Preview

The preview shows the whole publication as it will come out of the printer.

## 19.1 Opening the preview

The preview includes data and repeated pages, with no guides and no risk of accidental changes.
Open it with the [Preview] button on the editor's top bar (<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd>) or
from the [⋯] menu on the project card — then the preview opens straight away, without the editor.

![Preview on the top bar](k-podglad.png)

Pages follow in order, spreads stand side by side. Series pages have a caption, e.g. "3 of 31".
Clicking a page opens it in the editor; [Editor] or <kbd>Esc</kbd> takes you back to editing.

![Preview](podglad.png)

## 19.2 Zooming

- [−] and [+] on the bar; clicking the percentage goes back to 100%, i.e. the whole spread in the window.
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + mouse wheel zooms in at the cursor.
- On a phone or tablet: pinch with two fingers; a double tap zooms in or back out.


## 19.3 Presentation

[Presentation] shows one page or spread at a time, full screen. Arrow keys and the space bar
turn the pages, <kbd>Esc</kbd> or [×] closes it. On a phone, swipe sideways to turn
the page, swipe down to close the presentation.

## 19.4 3D mockup

[3D mockup] next to [Separations] on the preview bar shows the project as a printed object — like a product
photo in a studio: real page dimensions, thickness from the paper and page count, light with a shadow on a table
or wall. You can rotate it and flip through it before it goes to the print shop.

The full-screen button in the window header (next to close) enlarges the mockup to the whole screen — the
placement, page flipping and view stay. The same button or Esc returns to the normal window. It also works for someone
viewing the mockup from a link.

![3D mockup: an open hardcover book](k-makieta.png)

| Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Book – softcover** | Lies on a table. The cover comes from the cover section (→ 10.6): front, spine and back, at the size of the interior; stiff like card — it bends only at the spine. The interior page by page, open pages curved at the spine, page edges visible from the side. Without a cover section, the first page is the cover. |
| **Book – hardcover** | Rigid boards and a spine under a thin cover paper; at the hinge the board slopes smoothly down to the deepest point of the groove, with no sharp corners. When closed it lies against the block; when open the board turns in the hinge groove and lies at the same height as when closed. The cover spine (rounded or flat) is as wide as the block plus two boards and stays in place. Hinge width and board thickness come from the cover settings (→ 10.6). Endpaper: a sheet folded in half, glued to the board only where it lies on it; the piece between the fold and the board edge is loose (of fixed length). The free leaf of the endpaper is glued to the first page of the block along 3 mm at the spine: when open it curves back onto the board and slightly lifts the first page, and turning the first page lays it on the endpaper and flattens it. The block always has the same thickness. Nothing passes through the block or goes under the table. |
| **Saddle-stitched booklet** | Sheets folded in half, nested one inside another and stapled with two staples in the fold. No spine — all leaves come out of one place and only spread to their own height at the fold; the front edge is trimmed flush. Opens almost completely flat. Chosen for a cover with saddle-stitch binding. |
| **Spiral notepad** | Wire-O binding: two parallel wires at each hole. The ring diameter is chosen from the block thickness as manufacturers do (from 1/4″ to 1¼″, pitch 3:1 or 2:1); a block thicker than about 28 mm gets the largest ring and a note below the mockup. The notepad lies on the wire. |
| **Wall calendar** | Hangs on the wall; the Wire-O spiral at the top has a gap in the middle with a semicircular cut-out for the hanger. A turned leaf goes to the back. |
| **Desk calendar** | Stands on a desk on a thick cardboard stand; leaves turn over the top to the other side. |
| **Single sheet** | Flyer, poster, invitation — lies on a table, front and back. |

**Type matched to the project.** The **Type** list shows at the top only what the project really is — usually
one type, at most two: hard cover — **Book – hardcover**; soft — book or spiral notepad;
saddle-stitched — saddle-stitched booklet; no cover with 12–14 leaves, or a calendar — wall and desk calendar; one or two pages —
sheet; no cover, up to 64 pages in a multiple of 4 — notepad or saddle-stitched booklet; other without a cover — notepad or book. The saddle-stitched booklet warns you when the page count is not a multiple of 4 or when it is too thick for staples (over about 64 pages). The other types are lower down, in the **Doesn't match the project** group:
if you pick one of those, the mockup tells you what is missing and suggests what to do (e.g. change the binding to hardcover,
add a cover or start from a project template; missing leaves are shown as white).
| **Folded flyer** | From a flyer-type section (→ 10.11): folded with the cover on top, it unfolds panel by panel (roll fold or accordion), then turns over to the outside. |
| **Envelope** | From an envelope die line: front with the address, back with flaps and bottom, finally the open flap. |
| **Die-cut box** | From a box die line: first a flat printed sheet, then the walls, flaps and lid fold into a solid. |
| **Three-month calendar** | Hangs on the wall: a backboard with a header, three calendar pads and a red window on the middle one. |

When a section of the interior has a different format from the rest (e.g. an A4 endpaper with an A5 interior, with no type set),
the mockup can't assemble it into a book correctly and says so below the image – [Set section type…] opens that section's settings (→ 10.4).

The **Type** list shows only what fits the project's sections: flyer, envelope, box and three-month
calendar — when the project has such a section; books, notepad, calendars and sheet — when it has regular pages.
When the project has a **dust jacket** section, the hardcover book shows it on the cover (front, spine and back
from the jacket, flaps folded inside); the [Show dust jacket] switch hides it and reveals the cover.

The type is chosen automatically (with a cover — book; calendar from the name or from 12–14 leaves; one page — sheet);
you change it with the **Type** list. For a book or notepad, set the **Placement** list: **Lying** or **Standing** — closed, it stands
on its bottom edge; open, it spreads into a "V" like on a display shelf (the V opens smoothly together with the cover).
After you open a standing book, the view turns to face it head-on so both pages look the same, and after closing it
returns to an angle; if you rotate the view with your finger or mouse, it stays where you put it. The choice is remembered for the project.

On a phone or tablet the mockup keeps fewer pages in memory at once and draws them at a lower resolution (without
the shine of laminate and varnish in the light) – phones with little memory used to close the browser tab. If the tab still closes
with the mockup open, the next time you open it the mockup runs in **power-saving mode** and says so above the preview.

The interior format is the format of most pages in the project. Endpaper-type sections (front or back) are not part of
the interior: in a book the front endpaper lies on the inside of the front cover and as a loose leaf before
page 1, the back one — the other way round, just as after binding (an A4 landscape sheet with an A5 book splits into two halves). A hardcover without
endpapers in the project gets a blank endpaper. Any other page twice as wide as the interior — e.g. a spread on
a single sheet — also splits into two pages.

- **Rotating** — drag with the mouse or your finger. The light moves with the view, and the mockup can't be
  turned under the table or behind the wall.
- **Zooming** — mouse wheel, two fingers or [−] and [+]; [View] goes back to the front view. After zooming in,
  the pages load again at a higher resolution — they're sharp up close.
- **Page flipping** — the arrows below the mockup, the <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> keys or a tap on the right or left half of the image. Below it you can see what is open, e.g. "Pages 2–3" or "Leaf 3 of 13".
- [Download mockup image] saves a PNG image in the same framing as on screen — e.g. for a quote or a post
  online. You choose the size in the **Export** part (below). In the dark theme the studio background is dark.

Someone with a preview link (→ 23.1) also sees the mockup — in one variant, without settings. The link follows
your changes by itself: after you save a shot it shows the new variant, and a link created earlier gets the mockup the
next time the project is opened.

> Note: The mockup is drawn by the graphics card. A device without 3D graphics (an old phone, a blocked graphics card,
> e.g. Chrome on an older Mac) shows a **simplified mockup**: the same book, notepad or calendar, with the same
> pages, rotating with finger and mouse and page flipping, just without cast shadows, shine and reflections (there's
> a short note about it above the mockup). The mockup image (PNG) and saved shots work the same way.
> You can save a picture of the mockup only by downloading it — [Download mockup image] (PNG), also in a mockup from a link.

### Shot and lighting

The collapsed **Shot and lighting** part below the mockup gathers all the scene settings (the same on a phone):

| Group | What you set |
|---|---|
| **Camera** | rotation, tilt, distance and **lens** — a narrow angle is a telephoto lens (flat, no distortion), a wide one — wide angle with strong perspective |
| **Book** | opening angle (180° — flat), cover ajar, fanned pages and choice of spread from a list |
| **Light** | direction (from the left, from the right, from above, window — low from the side), tone (neutral, warm, cool), softness and shadow strength |
| **Surface and background** | studio gradient, solid color, wood, paper, concrete, marble, fabric, plaster or transparent; background color (with textures it changes their tint) |
| **Copies** | one, two or three side by side, a stack of 3 or 5, front and back |
| **Materials** | cover finish (as in the project, matte, gloss, soft-touch), white or cream paper, color of the spiral and stand; **Project foil and varnish in the light** — elements with a print finish (→ 22.2) shine like foil and varnish as you rotate the mockup |

[Restore defaults] returns to the starting settings. Light and background are remembered for the project on this device.

### Export and animation

In the **Export** part you choose the image size: **Web** (longer side 1600 px), **Print** (4000 px),
**Instagram** (square 1080 × 1080) or **Custom**, plus **Transparent background** — only the object remains,
with its shadow, ready to paste into a website or a presentation.

[Record animation] makes a short clip: **Turntable** or **Page flipping** (from the current page), as
**video** (MP4 if the browser can do it, otherwise WebM) or **GIF**. A progress bar shows the frames;
[Cancel] stops the recording.

### Saved shots

In the **Saved shots** part, type a name and tap [Save shot] — the type, camera, light, background,
copies and page positions are saved in the project (also on other devices and for the people you
work with; saving can be undone). Tapping a name in the list restores the shot, the bin deletes it.

## 19.5 Live preview

An open preview refreshes by itself when the project changes elsewhere — on another device, by a
collaborator or by AI. It stays at the same place and zoom; only the changed pages
are redrawn.

# 20. Preflight check

A list of things to check before you send the file to the print shop.

## 20.1 List of issues

[⋯] → [Preflight check] goes through the whole project — including every repeated page.

![Preflight check in the ⋯ menu](k-menu-kontrola.png)

The list has everything the warnings next to the page show (for the whole project at once), and more. Every printed page is checked after the data is filled in – with its own data: text with fields, an image from a column (the resolution of that page’s file), position, size and visibility from data, masters on left and right pages. Elements silenced with **Mute** are skipped. **Serious issues** – text that doesn't fit its frame, a table running off the page, a missing image file or font, a field without a value, missing data, a photo under 150 dpi – are in red and at the top of the list (the others, in orange, are sometimes intentional). With serious issues, PDF export asks: **Export anyway** or **Go back and fix**. The **Copy report** button (below the list; greyed out while texts on all pages are still being checked) copies all issues as text – with the project name, date and numbers – to paste into a message to the print shop, a note or a chat with AI.

| Issue | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Text doesn't fit in the frame** | On the listed pages the text sticks out of a fixed-height frame — e.g. with a longer value from the table. |
| **Field without a value** | A field with a typo or from a deleted column. |
| **Missing image file** | The table contains the name of an image that hasn't been uploaded. |
| **Duplicates** | The same value in a column marked **≠**, with page numbers. |
| **Values outside the list** | Values outside the list of a column marked **☰**, with row numbers. |
| **Low image resolution** | The image has less than 200 dpi in print (ideally 300) – it may come out blurry. Make it smaller on the page or upload a larger file. Doesn't apply to SVG drawings – they are vector, sharp at any size. |
| **Text too small** | Text smaller than 6 pt – hard to read in print, thin letter strokes may disappear. |
| **Line too thin** | A stroke thinner than 0.1 mm may not print. |
| **Missing font** | The text uses a font that isn't in the project or in Makietka – another one replaces it in the PDF; upload it in Project fonts. |
| **Text near the trim edge** | Text closer than 3 mm to the page edge – it may be cut off when trimmed. |
| **No bleed** | An element (background, bar, photo) reaches the page edge but not the bleed – a white strip may remain after trimming; drag it past the edge. |
| **Outside the page** | The element lies completely outside the page – it won't be printed. |
| **Beyond the bleed** | The element extends beyond the bleed – that part will be cut off. |
| **Into the margin** | The element reaches into the page margin (on the left or right page of a spread) – it may be trimmed or too close to the spine. An element running into the bleed (a background, a full-page photo) crosses the margin on purpose – no remark. |
| **Typesetting against Polish rules** | A conjunction at the end of a line, double spaces, a straight quote, a space before a comma, etc. — in every text frame (also ones that grow with the text) and on every repeated page, with the data from the table filled in. The same notes as in the editor's **Warnings** panel; **Mute** on an element skips them. |
| **Missing data** | An additional table ran out of rows before the end of the series. |
| **Series pages in spreads** | Where blank pages are, or from which page the block lands on the opposite side. |
| **Table goes off the page** | The rows grew with the content and the table went off the sheet. |
| **Incomplete signature** | A section is short of pages for a full signature (→ 10.7); with saddle-stitch binding — the page count isn't divisible by 4. |
| **Ink coverage** | An element's color, converted with the printer profile, gives over 300% ink — after you open the separations preview with a profile or export PDF/X-4 (→ 22.6). |
| **Element in the margin or outside the bleed** | As in the warnings list in the editor (→ 12.2). |

[Show] next to an issue opens the preview at the first page it concerns, with that
sheet highlighted. [Open template] opens the template page in the editor. Issues don't block export — the same list
is at the bottom of the PDF export window.

![List of issues](kontrola.png)

# 21. Export to PDF and PNG

A file for the print shop, for viewing on screen or for printing at home. The AI assistant can also create the file for you (→ 25.5).

## 21.1 Export window

Choose [⋯] → [Export to PDF…].

![Export to PDF in the ⋯ menu](k-menu-pdf.png)

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **File type** | **Vector – sharp text and shapes**: text with embedded fonts, sharp at any zoom, selectable. **Page images – page as a picture**: each page is a single image; it looks the same everywhere, but the file is larger. |
| **Range** | **Whole project**, **Selected sections** (tick one or several; each shows its page count) or **Selected pages** – numbers as in Word: `1-5, 8, 11-` (a comma or semicolon between parts, `11-` – from 11 to the end, `-3` – from the start to 3). Numbers count across the whole printout, including series repeats. The summary below tells you right away how many pages you'll get and hints when something can't be read. |
| **Purpose** | **For print – with bleed and crop marks** or **For viewing – trimmed to format**. Crop marks are added to sections that have them switched on (→ 10.4). |
| **Sheet layout** | **Regular pages** or pages laid out on sheets for printing yourself (→ 21.6). |
| **Quality** | Photo resolution: 300 dpi — the standard for print, 150 dpi — a small file for a test, 600 dpi — very sharp. |

At the bottom of the window there's the **To check before printing** list (→ 20). [Export] saves the file; you can stop a long
export with the [Cancel] button on the progress card.

A large file (from 150 pages, single file) is made by the Makietka server, not your device — the progress card shows
**Export to PDF – the Makietka server is making the file**, and the finished file downloads by itself. It looks the same, because the server
uses the same code. Exception: PDF/X with your own ICC profile (the profile file is only on your device)
is always made by the device. Without internet, or if the server has trouble, the device makes the file, as before.

## 21.2 Advanced

The **Advanced** part:

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Convert text to outlines (no fonts in file)** | Letters become shapes. Some print shops require this; the text can no longer be selected afterwards. |
| **Text and shape colors in CMYK (black as pure K)** | Colors saved as the four printing inks; black text is printed with black ink only, so it's sharp. |
| **Print standard** | **No standard – plain PDF**, **PDF/X-4 – coated paper (FOGRA39)**, **PDF/X-4 – uncoated paper (FOGRA47)** or **PDF/X-4 – printer's profile (.icc file)** — [Choose profile file…]. |
| **Photos in CMYK too (larger file)** | With PDF/X-4: photos converted to printing inks with the same profile. |
| **Split into files** | **Single file**, **Separate file for each section** or **Parts of a set number of pages** (**Pages per part**). You get several files in a ZIP archive. |
| **Include font files used in the project** | The download is a ZIP with the PDF file and a `czcionki` folder. |
| **PDF for on-screen reading: bookmarks and clickable links** | Bookmarks and links for on-screen readers (→ 21.7). On by default; not available with PDF/X-4 and sheet layouts. |
| **Print finishes** | When the project has them: **Separate plates in the same PDF**, **Separate PDF for each finish** or **Skip print finishes** (→ 22.4). |

## 21.3 How to choose settings for the print shop

Ask the print shop what it needs:

- it says "PDF/X-4" — choose that standard and the paper it will print on,
- it says "everything in CMYK" — also tick both CMYK boxes,
- it says "no fonts" or "outlines" — tick **Convert text to outlines**,
- it needs nothing special — **Vector**, **For print**, 300 dpi.

> Tip: A file from a project in screen colors looks slightly different in print. The safest option is to order
> a proof print.

## 21.4 What can't be saved as vectors

An element with a shadow, an element clipped by a mask and an SVG drawing with a filter go into the file as an image
of that one element — the rest of the page stays vector. Text in a font that can't be embedded
goes into the file as outlines. After export the app tells you if this has happened.

## 21.5 Page as PNG

[⋯] → [Other formats and import…] → [Download current page as PNG] saves an image of the open page at 300 dpi, with bleed —
e.g. to show someone in a message.

## 21.6 Booklet and several pages per sheet

**Sheet layout** in the export window places pages on sheets — for printing on an ordinary printer or at a small print shop:

| Layout | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Booklet to fold (saddle-stitched)** | Pages in pairs, so that after double-sided printing and folding the sheets in half you get a booklet. The page count is padded with blank pages to a multiple of 4. |
| **Booklet from signatures** | The same, but separately for each signature of **Signature (pages)** — 4, 8, 16 or 32 (by default as in the section settings, → 10.7). The signatures are sewn or glued together. |
| **2 pages per sheet**, **4 pages per sheet** | Consecutive pages side by side — e.g. A6 flyers, four to an A4 sheet. |

**Sheet**: **Fit to pages** (two A5 pages → a 296 × 210 mm sheet) or **A4** / **A3** — pages are centered and shrink if they don't fit. **Creep (mm)**: in a thicker booklet the middle leaves stick out after folding; the pages on them shift by part of this value towards the spine (e.g. 0.5–1 mm for 40 pages).


![Booklet: sheet plan in the export window](k-pdf-arkusze.png)
 With **For print** the sheets have crop marks, and a booklet — dashed fold marks. Pages on the sheets are trimmed to format, without bleed.

> Tip: It's easier to print a booklet on a home printer with [Print at home…] (→ 21.8).

## 21.7 PDF for on-screen reading

A regular PDF file comes with navigation for on-screen readers:

- **bookmarks** in the reader's side panel — the project's sections, with headings under them (book heading styles, → 13),
- **clickable table of contents** — a contents line takes you to its page,
- **cross-references** like "p. 12" take you to that page,
- **element link** — in the element panel, in the **Other** part, the **Link in PDF (web address, email)** field: clicking the element in the PDF opens the address; a QR code with a web address is clickable on its own.

Links are in vector files; bookmarks — in both file types. There's no navigation in PDF/X-4 for the print shop.

## 21.8 Print at home

Printing on an ordinary printer: [⋯] → [Print at home…] or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>P</kbd> in the editor and preview.
The window lays out the pages on sheets by itself — don't print the project from the browser menu.

![Print at home window](k-druk.png)

| Setting | What it means |
|---|---|
| **What to print** | **Regular print** (one page per sheet), **Booklet to fold** (sheets folded in half and stapled in the middle – the page order is arranged for you), **2** or **4 pages per sheet**. |
| **Paper** | A4, A3 or Letter – the sheet rotates by itself. |
| **Page size** | **Actual** (pages shrunk only when they don't fit) or **Fit to sheet**. |
| **Pages** | All, **selected sections** (one or several), **selected pages** (as in Word: `1-5, 8, 11-`) or just the current one. |
| **Grayscale** | Black-and-white print. |
| **Crop and fold marks** | For a booklet you'll trim after stapling. |

Below the settings you can see how the pages will be laid out on the sheets. [Print] opens the print dialog with the finished file
(on a phone the file opens in a new tab — choose **Share → Print** there), and [Download PDF for printing]
saves the same file.

The browser's print dialog may change the scale its own way, add margins or a header with the date and
address. Makietka prints a finished PDF file, not the page itself, so there are no headers or footers in it; 100%
scale and double-sided printing are saved in the file. Not every browser follows these settings, so the window
shows what to check in the printer dialog:

| In the printer dialog | Set |
|---|---|
| **Scale** | **100% / Actual size** — not "Fit" or "Shrink to page". |
| **Margins** | Default. The file already has margins, and the pages are laid out on the sheet. |
| **Headers and footers** | Off. If you see the date and address in the preview, untick this option. |

A printer can't print the few millimeters at the edge of the sheet. So a page the size of the sheet is slightly
reduced to leave a 5 mm margin; the window tells you by how much.

> Tip: The most reliable print: [Download PDF for printing] and print from a PDF program.
> The first time, print a [test page]: the 100 mm ruler should measure exactly 100 mm, and the 3, 5 and 10 mm frames
> show how much your printer leaves at the edge.

1. First print a single sheet as a test.
2. Check that the scale in the printer dialog is **Actual size / 100%**.
3. Print a booklet double-sided, **flip on short edge**; without double-sided printing — first
   the odd pages, then put the stack back in and print the even ones.
4. Fold the sheets in half (first one on top) and staple them in the fold.

# 22. Project colors, inks and finishes

## 22.1 Spot colors

Printing isn't just the four CMYK inks. Makietka knows spot colors (e.g. Pantone), finishes — varnish, foiling, embossing, white — and printing with black ink only or black plus one color. Everything goes into the PDF file the way the print shop expects: each ink on a separate plate.

A spot color is a separate, ready-mixed ink — in exactly the shade you need (e.g. a logo color). The easiest way is to add it at an element:

1. Select an element and open its color (e.g. **Fill color**).
2. At the bottom of the color picker: [Make this color a spot color…].
3. Type the ink name exactly as at the print shop or in the swatch book, e.g. "PANTONE 186 C", and [Add].

You can do the same in Properties → **Print finish** → **Spot ink** → **New from current color…**. You then pick the added color in any color field — fill, stroke, text, part of a text — in the **Spot colors** part, together with a **Tint** (e.g. 40%).

![Spot color in the color picker](k-kolor-dodatkowy.png)

You'll find the list of spot colors in [⋯] → [Project colors…]: there you can rename a color, enter **C**, **M**, **Y**, **K** (this is only the on-screen preview — the print shop will use its own ink) or delete it. Changing a spot color's CMYK changes all elements in that color. Deleting a color leaves the elements in an ordinary CMYK color.

## 22.2 Print finish: varnish, foiling, embossing, white

You set the finish at the element — nothing needs to be added beforehand. Select an element → Properties → **Print finish** and tap an effect:

| Effect | What it is | Plate in PDF |
|---|---|---|
| **Varnish** | Gloss varnish only in that spot, e.g. a title on a matte cover. | Spot_UV |
| **Foiling** | Metallic foil; **Foil color**: gold, silver or copper. | Foil_Gold, Foil_Silver, Foil_Copper |
| **Embossing** | A raised pattern pressed into the paper. | Emboss |
| **Debossing** | A recessed pattern pressed into the paper. | Debossing |
| **White** | White ink underneath — on colored, kraft or transparent stock. | White |

![Print finish in the element panel](k-uszlachetnienie.png)

Above the tiles you choose **how** to add the effect:

- **Color + effect** — the element prints in its own colors, and the effect goes on a copy of it exactly on top. This is how you put varnish on a photo or make colored text with foiling. This is the default for photos.
- **Effect only** — the element doesn't print with any ink, it's only the effect, e.g. text in pure gold. This is the default for shapes and text.

In the same part there's **Spot ink** — filling the element with a spot color (→ 22.1).

> Tip: A finish on very small text (below 6 pt) and thin lines (below 0.3 mm) may not reproduce well. **Preflight check** will remind you about it (→ 20).

**Cut-outs and folding pieces — die lines.** The same tiles also include technical lines. The element (shape,
line, closed pen shape, a whole group) stops being a drawing and becomes a line for the bindery or a plotter:

| Tile | What it is | In the editor | Plate in PDF |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cutting** | A contour to cut with a knife or plotter. For a group — each of its shapes. | red line | CutContour |
| **Crease** | A fold line — pressed in so the card folds evenly. | blue dashed | Crease |
| **Perforation** | A tear-off line (coupon, ticket). | orange dotted | Perforation |
| **Glue** | An area where glue goes (box or envelope flap). | green area | Glue |

The lines are always visible on the page but don't print with ink — in a vector PDF they go onto separate plates (CutContour
is recognized by most print & cut plotters). The **⋯** menu → [Other formats and import…] → [Plotter file (SVG, current page)] downloads just the
lines of the current page in millimeters — for Cricut, Silhouette or a print shop plotter (the line color says what to do).
**Preflight check** makes sure it can be cut and folded: a cut from an open line, a crease or glue with no cut
line, a crease or glue outside the cut-out piece. Ready-made die lines for envelopes, folders and boxes, with a 3D mockup, come from a section
of the **Die line** type (→ 10.4).

## 22.3 Print finish view

In the editor and preview a print finish appears as an effect: the shine of varnish, metallic foil, the shadow of embossing, white on a checkerboard. View menu → **Show print finishes** turns the effect off (→ 3.6).

## 22.4 Print finishes in the PDF file

In a vector file each print finish is a separate plate with **overprint** — it doesn't knock out the ink underneath. In the export window, in the **Advanced** part, choose **Print finishes**:

- **Separate plates in the same PDF** — the most common,
- **Separate PDF for each finish** — the main file without finishes and a separate file for each plate, in one ZIP,
- **Skip print finishes**.

A page-image file has no separate plates — choose a vector file.

## 22.5 Project colors and color printing — for the whole project or for a section

[⋯] → [Project colors…] gathers everything about colors in one window. At the top, in the **Applies to** field, you choose **Whole project** or a single section — e.g. a full-color cover with a black-and-white interior.

![The "Project colors" window for the "Interior" section](k-kolory-druku.png)

**Colors in the project** (or **in the section**) are the colors you use, with the number of uses. Tap a color and pick a new one — it changes everywhere in that scope: for the whole project on pages, in masters and in styles, for a section only on its pages. You can reverse the change with the usual [Undo].

**Color printing** says which inks the print will use:

| Mode | When |
|---|---|
| **Full color** | Regular CMYK printing (default). |
| **Grayscale** | Printing with black ink only — colors and photos come out in shades of black. |
| **B/W + 1 color** | Black and one spot color, e.g. a calendar with red Sundays. You choose the **Mode spot color** below. |
| **As in the project** | Sections only: the section prints the same way as the whole project (default). |

In the PDF each page is printed in its section's mode. In the color picker on a page of such a section, only shades of black and the mode color remain, and the section's colors are shown separately — **In this section**. The window shows colors outside the mode — [Replace with nearest allowed] changes them to black or the mode color. The button in **Preflight check** does the same.

At the bottom of the window, in the **Print finishes** part, you can see which effects are in the project and on how many elements.

> Tip: Extended CMYK (CMYK with extra inks, e.g. orange, green and violet) is done by the print shop itself from your file — you don't need a separate palette.

## 22.6 Separations preview

In the project preview, [Separations] shows the page split into the plates the print shop will print it with: **Cyan**, **Magenta**, **Yellow**, **Black**, spot colors and print finishes.

![Separations preview: a spot color and spot UV varnish with overprint](separacje.png)

1. Use the arrows above the image to move between pages.
2. Untick a plate to see the page without it; leave one and tick **Single plate in grayscale** — you'll see just that plate's ink (black = 100%).
3. **Show print finish overlay** — print finishes as a hatched overlay on top of the ink (they print with overprint and don't knock out the ink underneath).
4. **Highlight ink coverage above** **Coverage limit (%)** — places where the total ink exceeds the limit (usually 300% for coated paper) turn red. Below it is the highest coverage on the page.

**CMYK conversion**: **Simple** — like a vector file in CMYK without a profile (black and grays with black ink only; doesn't exceed 300%); **Coated paper (FOGRA39)** or **Uncoated paper (FOGRA47)** — the way the printer profile will convert it. After conversion with a profile, **Preflight check** will also point out element colors with ink coverage above 300% (→ 20).

# 23. Sharing via link

A preview link for people without an account — a client, family, the print shop.

## 23.1 Creating a link

Someone with the link sees the pages as in the preview; they can't change anything and can't see your other projects.
They can also open [3D mockup] (→ 19.4): rotate it, flip through it and download an image — in one variant, the same
as yours (the first saved shot, or with no shots — the type, placement and light from your mockup), without settings
and without changing the type. The mockup updates in the link by itself, also in links created before it was added.

1. In the preview click [Share] — or in the project list, [⋯] on the card → [Share via link…] (the project
   doesn't open, the pages are prepared in the background).
2. [Create link] — the link is ready right away; page images are prepared in the background (you can close the window). Anyone
   who opens the link in the meantime will see "The preview is being prepared", and the pages will appear for them by themselves.
3. Copy the link and send it.

The link is short: **makietka.app/** and six characters, e.g. `makietka.app/k7m2xq`. Links created earlier
get a short address by themselves the next time the project is opened; the old, long address still works.

![Share in the preview](k-udostepnij.png)
![Share preview window](udostepnianie.png)

A project with a link has a **Shared** badge in the project list — clicking it opens the same window.

## 23.2 Updating

The link updates by itself: a few seconds after your changes the app uploads the page images that have
changed. The address stays the same. [Update now] does it right away. Someone who has the link open
doesn't need to refresh the page — new pages appear by themselves.

The page images are prepared by the app, so the link keeps up with changes while the project is open —
in the editor or the preview, on any device. Meanwhile a bar is shown on screen:
**Updating the link preview – don't close Makietka**, with a page counter. You can close the Share window
— the pages keep being created, and the bar stays on screen. Just don't close the Makietka tab:
the browser will ask if you're sure. If the tab does close, the link will finish by itself the next time
you open the project.

When there are many pages to draw at once (from 40, e.g. the first link to a thick book or a watermark change),
the images are drawn by the Makietka server, not your device. The bar then changes to **The link preview is being drawn
on the server – you can close Makietka**: the link will finish by itself, even after you close the app. The server draws
the same images as the app. Small changes (a few pages) are still drawn by the device, because it only takes a moment.

## 23.3 Link protection

The **Link protection** part:

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Password – no one can see the preview without it** | Someone with the link first enters the **Link password**. After 10 wrong attempts the link stops accepting passwords for a quarter of an hour. |
| **Valid until a chosen date** | After the **Last day of validity** the link stops working. |

You can set protection when you create the link or at any time later: change the password, move the expiry
date, add protection to an ordinary link or remove it. The link address doesn't change. You can also open the same window
from the project list by clicking the link badge on the project card.

[Save protection] saves the settings. An empty password field with the password option ticked keeps the current one;
unticking both boxes removes protection. A date change alone takes effect immediately. A new password, or turning protection
on or off, prepares the link pages again from scratch — this takes a while, also from the project list (the project doesn't open).

## 23.4 Watermark

The **Watermark** part in the Share window: tick **Diagonal text on every page of the link**, type the **Watermark
text** (empty field — "PREVIEW"), set the **Opacity** slider (5–50 %, default 12 % — faint text; a small
preview under the slider shows it on a piece of the page right away) and click [Save watermark]. The text is baked into the link's page
images, also in the viewer's 3D mockup, so it can't be turned off on the viewer's side. The setting is saved immediately,
and the link pages are then prepared in the background (a bar on screen, see 23.2) — you can close the window;
unticking and saving removes the mark. The same applies to changing link protection. The watermark also works from the project list and doesn't change the printout or the PDF file.

![Watermark: text, Opacity slider and preview](k-znak-wodny.png)

## 23.5 3D mockup in the link

The **3D mockup in the link** part of the Share window decides what the viewer sees after tapping **3D mockup** in the link preview:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Viewer can see the 3D mockup** | Unticked — the link has only pages, no mockup. |
| **Type** | **As in the project mockup** (type matched to the project) or a chosen type, e.g. a notepad instead of a book. Types that don't fit are lower down, in the **Doesn't match the project** group. |
| **Shot** | The first saved shot or one chosen from the list of saved shots (→ 19.4): type, camera, light, background and page positions from that shot. |

**Save mockup in the link** saves your choice in the project; an existing link updates in the background. Also works from the project list.

## 23.6 Revoking a link

[Revoke link] deletes the shared pages — the old address stops working immediately. Sharing again
gives a new address.

> Note: The link is long and random, so it can't be guessed, but anyone who gets it can
> pass it on. Set a password for confidential projects.
