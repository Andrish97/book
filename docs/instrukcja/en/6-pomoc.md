# Part VI. Account, devices and help {ikona=motyw}

Your account and its settings, phone and tablet, keyboard shortcuts and a glossary.

# 27. Account

Your account stores your projects, fonts and elements, so you have them on every device.

## 27.1 Creating an account

On the sign-in screen, choose [New account] and enter:

- **Username** — 3–30 characters: letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-`. Others use this name to invite
  you to a project (→ 24.1).
- **Email**.
- **Password** — at least 8 characters, with a lowercase and an uppercase letter, a digit and a special character.

If you get a “Confirm your account” email, click the button in it — only then can you sign in.

![New account](rejestracja.png)
![Check your inbox](rejestracja-wyslano.png)

## 27.2 Signing in and password

In the **Email or username** field, enter your account address or your username (upper or lower case
doesn't matter), then your password. After 10 failed sign-in attempts with the same username, you have to wait fifteen minutes —
with your email address you can sign in right away.

[Forgot your password?] sends a link for setting a new password. The link works once; when it expires,
ask for a new one.

![Sign in](logowanie.png)

## 27.3 Account settings

Click the circle with your initial in the top-right corner of the project list and choose [Account settings].

![Account menu](k-menu-konta.png)

| Row | What you change |
|---|---|
| **Username** | The name others use to invite you to projects. |
| **Email** | Your account address. [Change email] sends a confirmation link to the new address; until you click it, you sign in with the old one. [Enter a different address] replaces the pending address with a new one. |
| **Password** | [Change password]. |
| **Theme** | How the app looks (→ 27.4). |
| **Notifications** | Notifications on this device (→ 27.6). |
| **Artificial intelligence** | Keys for AI assistants (→ 25.2). |
| **Brand kit** | Brand colors, fonts, text styles and logo — available in every project (→ 27.9). |
| **Storage** | How much space your files and projects take up (→ 27.5). |
| **Delete account** | Deleting your account (→ 27.7). |

[Sign out everywhere] (in the **Sign in** view) signs out all devices at once — e.g. after working
on someone else's computer.

![Account settings](konto.png)

## 27.4 Theme

The theme button is at the top of the screen (in the project list, in account settings, on the sign-in screen) and in the
editor's [⋯] menu. It shows the icon of the selected mode and opens a menu: **System**, **Light** or **Dark**.
You can choose the same in [Account settings] → **Theme**. The theme only changes how the
app looks — publication pages are always on white paper.

![Dark theme](motyw.png)

### Language

Next to the theme button there is a button with a flag – the **app language**: Polish (Polski), English or Ukrainian
(Українська). In the editor, the same choice is in the [⋯] menu → **Język / Language / Мова**. The first time, Makietka
uses your browser's language, then it remembers your choice on this device. The language only changes the app's labels —
project content (text on pages, names, data in tables) stays as it was entered. The help
(book button) and its screenshots also follow the chosen language.

## 27.5 Storage and limits

[Account settings] → **Storage** shows bars for your account limits:

| Limit | How much |
|---|---|
| Account files (images, fonts, link previews) | 2 GB |
| A single file | 50 MB |
| Projects | 100 |
| Images in one project | 1000 |

Below is a list of your projects: how much space each takes, how many images and pages it has, when it was last changed.
[Open] opens the project, [Show images] — its images window (→ 7.5). Other people's projects that
you have access to are listed separately and don't count toward your limits.

From 80% of a limit, the app warns you — here and when you upload images. [Clean up…] shows how much
space it will free and deletes only files no project uses any more: old versions of replaced
photos, files of deleted projects and of disabled links. Files from the last two days stay.

## 27.6 Notifications

Notifications tell you right away about invitations and comments — even when the app is closed.
In [Account settings] → **Notifications**,
turn on **On this device** (the browser will ask for permission) and choose what you want to hear about: invitations,
replies to your invitations, role and access changes, comments and mentions (→ 24.7). Clicking a
notification opens the right project or thread.

You turn notifications on separately on each device. On iPhone and iPad they only work
in the installed app (→ 28.4).

**By email** — a switch for the whole account: an email when someone invites you to a project or
shares it with you. When it's off, invitation emails don't arrive, and the invitation waits in the app
([Projects] → [Invitations]). Account emails (address confirmation, new password) can't be turned off.

![Notifications in account settings](k-powiadomienia.png)

## 27.7 Deleting your account

[Account settings] → **Delete account** deletes your account and all your projects. Before you do that,
export the projects you want to keep (→ 2.6).

> Note: Deleting your account can't be undone.

## 27.8 Contact

A question, an idea or a bug? [Contact] is in the account menu and below the sign-in form. [Attach files]
adds a screenshot, a project file or a PDF (up to 5 files, 10 MB in total). You can also write
to kontakt@makietka.app.

![Contact form with an attachment](k-kontakt.png)

## 27.9 Brand kit

You save the colors, fonts and logo of your company (or school, parish, club) once — in your account — and have them
in every project: [Account settings] → **Brand kit** → [Edit brand kit…] (or Library →
**Brand**).

**Several kits** — e.g. one for each client, school or publisher. At the top of the brand window is the **Kit** list:
[New kit…] (empty), [Copy…] (with the current kit's colors, fonts, styles and logo), [Rename…], [Delete]. Changes
to a kit are saved when you switch to another one. The choice in account settings is the **kit in use** — the brand of projects
that don't have their own. A choice made with a project open (the brand window from the Library or from the ⋯ menu) is remembered by the project:
its colors, fonts and Library show that kit, while other projects keep theirs. [Apply brand to project…]
does the same — in its window you choose which kit to apply.

- **Brand colors** — with a name, e.g. “Brand navy”. The **Pantone / CMYK** switch turns a color into a spot
  color, printed with a separate ink (→ 22.1).
- **Brand fonts** — one for headings, one for body text.
- **Text styles** — [Add preset styles] adds Title, Subtitle, Heading 1–4, Paragraph, Highlighted paragraph, Caption,
  Quote, List, Footnote, Footer / running head and Label in the brand fonts and color; [Custom style] — more, with no
  limit. Each style has a font, size, weight, italic, line spacing, letter spacing, color and letter case.
- **Logo** — PNG, JPG, WebP or SVG; you insert it from the Library.

![Brand text styles](k-marka-style.png)


![Brand kit](k-marka.png)

The brand is at hand in the color window (→ 6.7), at the top of the font list (→ 5.8) and in the Library (→ 4.8).

- **color picker** — the **Brand** part above “Used in project” (→ 6.7),
- **font list** — the **Brand** group at the very top (→ 5.8),
- **Library** — the **Brand** category: logo, [Apply brand to project…] and [Edit brand kit…] (→ 4.8),
- [⋯] → [Apply brand to project…] — replaces the colors and fonts of the whole project with brand ones (→ 3.3).

**Apply brand to project.** The window shows every color and every font in the project with a suggestion:
a color gets the closest brand color (black, white and grays stay), the most common font gets the body font,
the others get the heading font. In the lists you can change a suggestion or choose “no change”. The
**Text styles** part copies the brand styles into the project styles (→ 5.4): a project style with the same name (e.g. “Title”)
gets the brand look and stays linked to its texts — they change right away — and missing styles are added.
[Apply] changes pages, masters and styles at once; you undo it all with a single [Undo] (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>).

# 28. Phone and tablet

How to work on a touch screen and how to install the app.

## 28.1 Gestures

On a touch screen:

| Gesture | What it does |
|---|---|
| **One finger** | Selects and moves elements. |
| **Two fingers** | Move the view and zoom. |
| **Quick swipe sideways** | On an empty part of the page, when the whole page fits on the screen: left – next page, right – previous page (with spreads, the whole spread). A slow drag selects with a frame as usual. |
| **Double tap** | Like a double click: typing in text and table cells, entering a group, editing points. |
| **Press and hold** | Shows a tip, e.g. the description of the save cloud. |

In the preview, pinching zooms, and in a presentation, swiping sideways turns the page (→ 19).

## 28.2 Editor on a phone

On a phone, the editor works in portrait. You open the **Pages**, **Properties**, **Layers** and
**Warnings** panels with buttons at the bottom of the screen. A panel slides up from the bottom, and the view
moves by itself so the selected element is visible above the panel.

**Handle** (the bar at the top of the panel): drag it up to enlarge the panel, down to
shrink or close it. Tapping the handle shrinks an enlarged panel to its normal height, and closes a normal
one. Tapping the panel's button again also closes it.

Less common tools — polygon, drawing, pen, comment, copy formatting — are under [⋯] at the end of the
insert bar (→ 3.4). There are no rulers or frame cropping on a phone: you enter guides as numbers (→ 12.4),
and crop images with numbers in the properties (→ 7.2).

A phone held sideways shows a “Not enough vertical space” screen — turn it to portrait.

![Editor on a phone](telefon.png@telefon)

## 28.3 Bottom sheets

On a phone and tablet, windows (Data, Masters, Export to PDF, Project images and others) slide up from the
bottom of the screen. Instead of an X they have a **handle** — drag it down or tap it to close;
tapping the dimmed background also closes them. The window's main button is at the bottom, always visible.
Dropdown lists and color and date pickers, which also slide up from the bottom on a phone, have the same handle —
tapping outside them or on the handle hides them. Small menus (⋯, view) drop down below their button
and hide when you tap next to them.

![Window on a phone](telefon-zaslonka.png@telefon)

## 28.4 Installing the app

Installed Makietka opens from the home screen like a regular app — full screen,
without the browser bar.

| Device | How to install |
|---|---|
| **Android** (Chrome) | [Install the app] in the account menu or in the browser menu ⋮. |
| **iPhone and iPad** (Safari) | [Install the app] shows the steps: **Share → Add to Home Screen**. |
| **Computer** (Chrome, Edge) | The install icon in the address bar or [Install the app]. In Safari on a Mac: **File → Add to Dock**. |

The installed app on a computer opens `.makietka` files with a double click.

## 28.5 Full screen

[Full screen] (four corners) in the project list, in the editor and in the preview hides the browser's
bars; <kbd>Esc</kbd> or the same button brings them back. On iPhone the browser doesn't allow
full screen — there you get it by installing the app (→ 28.4).

## 28.6 Longer tasks

When something takes more than a moment — e.g. opening a large project, exporting a PDF, printing at home, uploading
images — a card with a progress bar appears at the top, and when it's known how much is left, with a counter, e.g. “17 of 48”.
You can stop a PDF export with the [Cancel] button. Page thumbnails of a large project are drawn in the background (a thin bar
in the Pages panel) — you can keep working normally in the meantime.

![Progress card during PDF export](k-postep.png)

# 30. Glossary

## 30.1 Printing and print-ready files

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CMYK** | The four printing inks: cyan, magenta, yellow, black. The print shop converts on-screen colors (RGB) to CMYK. |
| **Dpi** | The number of image dots per inch at print size. A photo for print should have at least 300 dpi. |
| **Imposition** | Arranging pages on a print sheet so that after folding and trimming they're in the right order (e.g. a booklet). |
| **Spot color (Pantone)** | A ready-mixed ink of an exact shade, printed separately alongside CMYK. |
| **Overprint** | Ink printed on top of the ink below without knocking it out — important for black text and finishes. |
| **PDF/X-4** | A PDF variant for print shops: embedded fonts, a stored color profile, transparency without flattening. |
| **Ink coverage** | The total of CMYK inks in one spot (in %). Too much (usually over 300%) means the ink won't dry. |
| **Color profile (ICC)** | A description of the print shop's press and paper; when converting to CMYK it gives colors as close as possible to the intended ones. |
| **Resolution** | See Dpi. |
| **Separations** | Separate printing plates for each ink (C, M, Y, K, spot colors, finishes). |
| **Bleed** | A few millimeters beyond the edge of the page; backgrounds and photos extend all the way there, and the print shop trims it off. |
| **Imposed copy (n-up)** | One copy of a small item (business card, sticker) on a print sheet – many of them fit on one sheet. |
| **Vector** | A drawing described by shapes, not pixels — sharp at any zoom (text, shapes, vector PDF). |
| **Crop marks** | Lines in the PDF file that show the print shop where to cut. |

## 30.2 Print finishing

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **White underprint** | A layer of white ink under the colors — needed when printing on transparent, colored or metallic material. |
| **Score, scoring** | A crease pressed into card (e.g. a cover) so that it folds evenly and doesn't crack. |
| **Fold** | The line along which a sheet is folded (e.g. a trifold leaflet); on card it's preceded by a score. |
| **Spot varnish** | Glossy varnish only on selected areas of the page, e.g. on the title. |
| **Perforation** | A line of small cuts that makes it easy to tear off part of a sheet, e.g. a calendar page. |
| **Embossing** | A raised or recessed pattern pressed into the paper. |
| **Section type** | What a section is in the publication (book block, cover, endpaper, insert, leaflet…) – it sets its format, numbering and place in the spine. |
| **Finish** | Extra print finishing: varnish, foil, embossing, white underprint. |
| **Die line** | The shape along which the print shop cuts and scores the sheet (envelope, box, sticker); in the PDF a separate CutContour color. |
| **Foil stamping** | Shiny foil (gold, silver, colored) transferred onto paper in selected places. |

## 30.3 Paper and binding

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Book block** | All the inside pages of a book together, without the cover. |
| **Booklet** | A few sheets folded in half and stapled at the spine; the pages have to be arranged on the sheets accordingly (imposition). |
| **Format** | The page size after trimming, e.g. A5 = 148 × 210 mm. |
| **Paper weight** | Paper thickness given as the weight of a square meter (g/m²) — among other things, the spine width depends on it. |
| **Spine** | The part of the cover between the front and the back; its width depends on the number of pages and the paper. In a spread — where the pages are bound. |
| **Headband** | A strip of fabric at the top and bottom of the spine of a hardcover book block. |
| **Dust jacket** | A paper cover put on a hardcover, with two flaps folded inside (→ 10.11). |
| **Binding** | How the pages are joined: saddle-stitched (staples), perfect bound (softcover), hardcover, spiral. |
| **Spread** | The left and right pages side by side, as in an open book. |
| **Signature** | A sheet folded into several leaves — a book is sewn from signatures of 4, 8, 16 or 32 pages. |
| **Flap** | The part of a dust jacket or cover folded inside behind the cover board. |
| **Ribbon (bookmark)** | A narrow ribbon glued into the spine of a hardcover, for marking your place in the book. |
| **Insert** | Pages on different paper bound into the middle of a book, e.g. photos on coated paper – without page numbers. |
| **Endpaper** | A sheet joining the book block to the hard cover: one half glued to the inside of the cover, the other half is the first (or last) loose leaf. |

## 30.4 Text and typography

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Line spacing** | The distance between consecutive lines of text. |
| **Justification** | Aligning text to both edges of the frame — the lines are of equal length. |
| **Baseline** | The invisible line the letters “stand” on. A baseline grid aligns lines in neighboring columns and across pages. |
| **Margin** | The strip along the edge of the page where no text is placed. |
| **Style** | A saved text look, changed in all texts at once. |
| **Widow and orphan** | A single line of a paragraph left alone at the end or the beginning of a column or page. |
| **Running head** | Text at the top or bottom of the page repeated on many pages, e.g. the chapter title. |

## 30.5 Editing and tools

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Element** | Anything that sits on a page: text, shape, line, image, table, group. |
| **Gradient** | A smooth transition between colors in a fill, also into transparency. |
| **Cropping** | Choosing the part of a photo that is visible in the frame. |
| **Opacity** | The transparency of an element or color: 100% — fully visible, 0% — invisible. |
| **Mock-up** | A page-by-page design of a publication; 3D mock-up — a preview of the printed item (book, calendar). |
| **Soft edges** | The edge of a photo or shape smoothly fading into transparency. |
| **Guide** | A helper line on the page for aligning elements; it doesn't print. |

## 30.6 Project and data

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Block (of a series)** | A page or a few neighboring pages of a series, repeated together. |
| **Field** | A name in braces, e.g. `{dzien}`; on the finished page it turns into a value. |
| **Document field** | A field that comes from the project: page number, section, date. |
| **Section** | A part of the publication with its own format, margins and numbering, e.g. the cover and the inside. |
| **Series** | Repeating a page or block for consecutive table rows. |
| **Data table** | A sheet in the project that fields and series take their values from — not to be confused with a table on a page. |
| **Skipped row** | A `[skip]` row: it takes up a place in the block, but its page isn't created. |
| **Master** | Elements shared by many pages: background, footer, page number. |

## 30.7 AI

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AI assistant** | A chat program (e.g. Claude, ChatGPT) that, once the connector is added, reads and changes your projects (→ 25.1). |
| **AI key** | Permission for the assistant: which projects it can access, whether it can only read, and how long it's valid (→ 25.2). |
| **Connector** | An address with your key through which the AI assistant works in your projects. You see it only once, when you create the key. |
| **Key scope** | Selected projects or full access to all of them, including creating and deleting projects. |
