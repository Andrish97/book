# 4. Elements on the page

Everything on a page is an element: here is what all of them have in common — selecting, moving, groups, ready-made elements from the Library.

## 4.1 Inserting

Click a tool on the insert bar (→ 3.4) — the element appears in the middle of the view and is
selected. You can also drag an image from your disk onto the page or paste it from the clipboard
(<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd>).

Master elements (→ 11) are visible on the page but can't be selected here — you change them in the master.

![Inserted text](wstawianie.png)

## 4.2 Selecting

- **A click** selects an element.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd> + click adds an element to the selection or removes it.
- **Dragging a box** from an empty spot on the page selects the elements it touches.
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd> selects all elements on the page.
- To select an element lying under another one, use the layers list (→ 9).
- **Double-click**: in text — typing, in a group — entering the group, in a table cell —
  typing in the cell, in a pen shape — editing points, in an image — cropping.
- <kbd>Esc</kbd> clears the selection.

## 4.3 Moving, size and rotation

- **Drag** an element to move it. Arrow keys move it by 1 mm, with <kbd>Shift</kbd> — by 10 mm.
- **The circles at the corners and sides** change the size, **the circle above the element** rotates it.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd> while dragging — only horizontally or vertically; while rotating — in 15° steps.
- <kbd>Alt</kbd> while dragging — you drag a copy.

You can type exact values in the properties, in the **Position and size (mm)** part:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **X**, **Y** | The top-left corner of the element, in mm from the top-left corner of the page (without bleed). |
| **Width**, **Height** | Size in mm. The padlock between them keeps the proportions. |
| **Rotation** | Angle in degrees. |

With several elements selected, the numbers refer to their shared bounding box.

## 4.4 Aligning and even spacing

The **Align** part in the properties:

- **one element** — aligns to the page margins: left, center, right, top,
  vertical center, bottom,
- **several elements** — aligns them to each other,
- **three or more** — additionally [Distribute horizontally] and [Distribute vertically]: equal spacing between elements.

While moving, an element “snaps” to the edges and centers of other elements, and arrows
show distances in mm (→ 12.5).

![Aligning several elements](wyrownanie.png)

## 4.5 Groups

Select several elements and choose [Group] (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd>). A group moves,
scales and rotates as one element. [Ungroup] (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd>)
splits it back apart.

**An element of a group without ungrouping:** double-click the group — it opens (orange
dashed frame) and the element under the cursor is selected. You can move it, change it in the
properties or type in it. Clicking another element of the group selects that one, <kbd>Shift</kbd>
adds more. <kbd>Esc</kbd>, a click outside the group or [Back to the whole group] closes it.

- In an open group, [Duplicate] leaves the copy in the group, and [Delete] deletes only the selected element.
- A group inside a group opens with another double-click; <kbd>Esc</kbd> goes back up one level.

## 4.6 Order, locking and the clipboard

- The **Order** part: [Bring to front], [Higher], [Lower], [Send to back]. You can do the same by dragging
  in the layers list (→ 9).
- The **Other** part:
  - **Opacity** — the element's transparency in %,
  - **Lock position and size** — you won't move the element by accident; you can still change color and text,
  - **Mute warnings for this object** — e.g. for a background that deliberately extends into the bleed,
  - **Name** — the element's name in the layers list.
- [Copy to clipboard] and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> on another page paste the element in the same
  spot. The page properties also have [Paste copied elements].
- **Between projects**: the copied element waits in this device's clipboard — open another project
  and paste (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> or [Paste copied elements]). The element's images go to the images
  of the new project. The clipboard survives switching to another project, reloading and a second tab.
- **Pages and sections** to another project: page menu (⋯ on the thumbnail) → [Copy to clipboard (also to another
  project)], section menu → [Copy section to clipboard (also to another project)]. In the other project: page menu → [Paste pages from clipboard]
  (after this page, in its section) or section menu → [Paste section from clipboard] (after this section). Together with the pages
  come their images, masters, series with data tables and text styles. The menu item tells you what is on the clipboard
  and from which project. You can't paste a cover into a project that already has one — paste just the pages instead.

## 4.7 Shadow

The **Shadow** part works for every element:

**Drop shadow under the element** turns the shadow on. You can set its color, **Strength**, offset **Right** and **Down**
in mm (negative — left or up) and the edge **Blur** in mm.

## 4.11 Photo collage

A collage is a ready layout of photo fields. Drop a photo from your computer onto a field – it goes into that field. You can also double-click the collage, choose a field and insert an image from the project in the **Image in shape** part (→ 7.7).

In the collage panel you can change the **Layout** (fields are added or removed, photos in the first fields stay), **Spacing between photos**, **Corner rounding**, **Width** and **Height**.

## 4.12 QR code and barcode

Insert a code from [Library], the **Codes** category. Set the rest in the **Code** part of the panel:

| Setting | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Type** | **QR code** or **EAN-13 / ISBN barcode** (e.g. the ISBN on a book cover). |
| **What it contains** (QR) | **Website address**, **Text**, **Email**, **Phone** or **Wi-Fi network**. |
| **Error correction** (QR) | How much of the code can be covered: from **Low (7%)** — the smallest code — to **Highest (30%)**, e.g. under a logo. |
| **EAN or ISBN number** | The check digit is added automatically; you can type an ISBN with hyphens. |
| **Size** | [Nominal size (100%)] sets the EAN code to 37.29 × 25.93 mm. |
| **Code color**, **Background**, **Quiet zone** | The code must be dark on a light background, with an empty band around it. |

A data field in the content — e.g. `{adres}` or `{isbn}` — gives a different code on each series page (→ 15).

If the code is too small, too light or has wrong content, the panel and **Preflight check** will remind you.

## 4.13 Copy style (format painter)

Transfer the look of one element to another with one click — without retyping colors and weights.

[Copy style] (brush) is in the properties panel header, in [⋯] next to the element in the layers list,
and on a phone also under [⋯] on the insert bar (→ 3.4).

![Copy style in the panel header](k-malarz.png)

1. Select an element that already looks right.
2. Click [Copy style] (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd>).
3. Click the element that should get this look.

- **Double-click** [Copy style] — continuous mode: you click one element after another until you press <kbd>Esc</kbd>.
- **Part of a text:** in the text you're typing, select words and click [Copy style] — only the selected
  part gets the look. You can copy the look from a part of a text the same way.

Everything to do with the look is transferred — colors, gradients, stroke, font, spacing, photo filters,
opacity, shadow, print finishes — and the content stays. Tables, codes, charts and calendars have their own settings
and don't take on the look.

## 4.14 Speech bubble

A speech bubble is one element: a text frame with a bubble around it. Insert it from [Library], category **Speech bubbles**. You type in it like in an ordinary text frame – the bubble grows with the text.

| Type | Look |
|---|---|
| **speech** | Oval with a tail. |
| **thought** | Cloud with small circles instead of a tail. |
| **shout** | Burst with spikes. |
| **whisper** | Oval with a dashed outline. |
| **narration** | Rectangular box without a tail. |

- Drag the orange handle on the page to move the tip of the tail – put it at the character's mouth. The tail moves together with the bubble.
- In the **Speech bubble** part of the panel you change the **Type**, turn the **Tail** on or off, and set **Fill**, **Outline** and **Outline width**. Font, size and text colour work as in ordinary text (→ 5).
- The bubble comes out in the PDF as vectors, just like on the page.

# 5. Text

Text frames, their look, styles and typesetting — from a single caption to the text of a book flowing across many pages.

## 5.1 Typing

Insert text with the [Text] button (<kbd>T</kbd>) and start typing right away. Double-click existing text
to type in it; <kbd>Esc</kbd> ends typing. <kbd>Enter</kbd> starts a new paragraph.

Change the frame width with the side handles — the text reflows, the letters don't stretch.
The frame height grows with the text unless you set a fixed height (→ 5.6).

![Text on the page](wstawianie.png)

## 5.2 Text look

Properties of the selected text:

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Style** | A saved text look (→ 5.4). |
| **Font** | The typeface. In the list, start typing a name to find it (→ 5.8). |
| **Size** | In points (pt). The circle on the right is the **text color**. |
| [B] [I] [U] [S] | Bold, italic, underline, strikethrough. Grayed out when the font has no such variant. |
| **Alignment** | Left, center, right, justified. |
| **Line spacing** | Space between lines; 1 is single. |
| **Letter spacing** | Spreads letters apart; a negative value tightens the text. |
| **Letter case** | [AA] — all caps, [aa] — all lowercase, [Aᴀ] — small caps: this is only the look, the content stays as typed. [Aa] — “sentence case”: changes the content itself. |
| **List** | Bullets, dashes or numbers before each paragraph (→ 5.5). |
| **Letter outline** | Color and **Letter stroke weight** — an outline around each letter. |

![Text properties](wlasciwosci.png)

## 5.3 Part of a text

Double-click the text, select a part and change it with the same settings — the panel shows
“You're changing only the selected text.” This is how you make one word bold or change the color of one line.
The look of a part containing a field (→ 15) carries over to the inserted value.

## 5.4 Styles

A **style** is a saved text look, e.g. “Title”, “Caption”, “Bubble”. Apply it to all texts
that should look the same.

- The **Style** list in the properties applies a style to the selected text.
- [New style] saves the look of the selected text under a new name.
- When you change a text that has a style, [Update style] appears — it changes all texts with that style
  in the whole project — and [Restore] — it goes back to the style's look.
- A style also saves the **Paragraph composition** settings (→ 5.9) — e.g. hyphenation for the whole text of a book.

A **character style** is a saved look of a part of a text, e.g. “Emphasis” or “Proper name”.

1. Select part of a text (double-click the text and select the words).
2. Set its look (→ 5.3), then in the **Text style** part choose [New character style].
3. Select other parts and choose the style from the **Character style** list. With nothing selected, the whole text gets the style.

[Update style] moves the look of the selection into the style — all parts with that style
in the project change. Deleting a character style doesn't change the look of the parts.

## 5.5 Lists

The **List** list in the text properties: **Bullets**, **Dashes** or **Numbers**. The list mark stands before
each paragraph, and the following lines of the paragraph line up under the first. In text flowing through several frames,
numbering continues. Shortcuts: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>8</kbd> (bullets) and
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>7</kbd> (numbers).

## 5.6 Text frame

The **Text frame** part is useful when the text should take a fixed space — e.g. texts from data of varying
length (→ 17.6):

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fixed frame height** | The frame doesn't grow with the text. Type the height in mm or change it with the top or bottom handle. |
| **Vertical alignment** | Text at the top, middle or bottom of the frame. |
| **Shrink text when it doesn't fit** | The font gets smaller until the text fits — but not below the **minimum size**. |

Text that still doesn't fit is cut off at the bottom of the frame and goes on the warnings list.

![A fixed-height frame](ramka-tekstu.png)

## 5.7 Columns and text across several frames

Longer text can flow through several frames: whatever doesn't fit in the first one moves on to the
next. The **Text flow** part:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| [Split into columns] | Turns the frame into 2, 3 or 4 columns side by side. |
| [Add continuation frame] | A new frame next to it (or below it) that the rest of the text flows into. |
| [Continued on next page] | A frame on the next page — with a new page if needed. |
| [Link text frames] | Links two selected frames. |
| [Unlink frames] | Each frame keeps its own part of the text. |
| [Previous frame], [Next frame] | Go to the neighboring frame, also on another page. |

You type in any frame, and the text reflows as you go. The text look (font, size, list)
is shared by the linked frames. A purple line shows where the text from the selected frame flows.

**Long text across many pages (a book).** Turn on **Add pages until all the text fits** — when the text doesn't
fit in the last frame, new pages are added after its page (in the same section, with its masters) with a frame
in the same place and size. When the text gets shorter, empty added pages at the end disappear. Pages are added after
you finish typing (Esc or a click outside), after pasting and after changing the text look.

**A paragraph on a new page** (e.g. a chapter): put the cursor in the paragraph and turn on **Start this paragraph on a new page** — the paragraph
and everything after it moves to the next frame. You choose this for each paragraph separately: a chapter can start
on a new page or keep flowing. With page adding on, the next frame is created automatically.

## 5.9 Paragraph composition

The collapsible **Paragraph composition** part in the text properties decides how the text breaks into lines.
You can save the same settings in a style (→ 5.4).

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Polish typesetting rules** | On in new texts. A line doesn't break after an abbreviation (np., tj., ul., dr…), after an initial (J. Kowalski), between a number and a unit, a date or further digits (5 kg, 12 maja, 10 000), and a dash doesn't start a line — as in the Polish standards PN-83/P-55366 and PN-70/P-55036. With **Hyphenate**: at most 3 hyphenated lines in a row, at least 2 letters before the hyphen and 3 after it, no hyphenation of a paragraph's last word or of capitalized words (a word longer than the line is always hyphenated). In justified text, spaces may also tighten slightly (up to 20 %), so there are fewer lines with gaps. In text flowing through frames, a title (a short paragraph in larger or bold type) isn't left alone at the end of a frame — it moves on with the next paragraph, and the frames on the left and right page of a spread end at the same height (column balancing: when widows or a title shortened one, the other is shorter too). Under the switch there are notes about places that break the rules and a **Fix characters** button: curly quotes „…” instead of straight ones, an en dash (–) instead of a hyphen between spaces, an ellipsis (…) instead of three dots, no double spaces and no spaces before a comma — formatting of parts of the text stays. |
| **Break the whole paragraph at once** | On in new texts. The lines of a paragraph are chosen together, not one by one — like the “paragraph composer” in DTP programs. The program weighs all possible break points and picks the layout with the most even word spacing and as few hyphenated words as possible; the Polish typesetting rules still apply. You see it best in justified text: instead of one line with big gaps, the spacing is spread over several lines. A paragraph that wraps around another element, has a drop cap or tabs is broken line by line. |
| **Hyphenate** | Long words are hyphenated at the end of a line according to Polish rules — justified text then has no gaps. Next to it you set the shortest word to hyphenate and how many letters stay before and after the hyphen. |
| **Conjunctions at line end** | Single-letter **a, i, o, u, w, z** (or also two-letter ones: do, na, że…) move to the next line together with the following word, as in Polish typesetting. |
| **Widows and orphans** | Text flowing through several frames (→ 5.7) doesn't leave a lone line of a paragraph at the bottom or top of a frame. |
| **Align to baseline grid** | Lines sit on the section's grid lines (→ 5.13). |
| **Don't wrap around other elements** | The text lies over elements with text wrap instead of flowing around them (→ 5.12) — e.g. a caption on a photo. |

![Paragraph composition in the text properties](k-sklad-akapitu.png)

**Typesetting warnings.** Text that breaks the Polish rules (a conjunction or abbreviation at the end of a line, a number separated from its unit, a dash at the start of a line, more than 3 hyphenated lines in a row, a hyphenated last word of a paragraph, a very short last line of a paragraph, a loose line, a straight quote, three dots, double spaces…) shows up in the editor warnings and in the preflight check (→ 20.1) with an example of the spot. To silence a warning for intentional text, use the **Mute** button.

## 5.10 Drop cap

A drop cap is an enlarged first letter of a paragraph spanning several lines. In the **Paragraph composition** part, choose
**Drop cap**: **2 lines** … **5 lines**. The letter extends from the top edge of the first line
to the baseline of the last one, and the text wraps around it. The **Drop cap font** and **Drop cap color** can differ
from the text.

The drop cap goes on the first paragraph of the text. In text flowing through several frames — only in the first frame.

![Drop cap, hyphenation, dotted tab stops and text wrapping around a circle](sklad.png)

## 5.11 Tab stops

The <kbd>Tab</kbd> key in the text you're typing inserts a tab: the text after it jumps to the nearest position.
Without set positions, tab stops are every 12.5 mm.

In the **Paragraph composition** part, [Add tab stop] adds a position. Each one has:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tab stop N** | Position in mm from the left edge of the frame. |
| **Alignment** | **left**, **center**, **right** or **decimal comma** (numbers with a comma line up one under another). |
| **Fill** | Empty, dots, dashes or a line before the text — e.g. a table of contents “Chapter 1 . . . . . 12”. |

![A right tab stop with dots](k-tabulatory.png)

## 5.12 Text wrap

A photo, shape or group can push text away. Select the element and in the **Other** part choose **Text wrap**:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **text wraps around the element's frame** | Lines end before the element's rectangle (or start after it). |
| **text wraps around the shape** | For an ellipse, polygon and pen shape — lines follow its edge. |
| **text jumps below the element** | There is no text next to the element; it continues below it. |

**Text offset from element** is the free space around the element (2 mm by default). Text wrap affects
all texts on the page — also while you're moving the element. The text picks the side of the element
where it has more room.

![Text wrap in the Other part](k-oblewanie.png)

## 5.13 Baseline grid

A baseline grid puts the lines of all columns and pages at the same height — as in books
where the lines on both sides of a sheet match when held up to the light.

1. In the section settings (→ 10.4), turn on **Baseline grid in this section**: **Spacing** (usually the line spacing
   of the body text) and **First line** (mm from the top edge of the page, usually the top margin).
2. In the view menu (→ 3.6), **Baseline grid** shows these lines on the page.
3. For texts that should sit on the grid, turn on **Align to baseline grid** (→ 5.9) — preferably in the body
   text style.

## 5.14 Text on an arc, circle and path; warping

Select the text and in the **Text shape** part of the panel choose a **Shape**. You'll also find ready examples in [Library], in the **Texts** category.

![Text on an arc, in a circle and warped](k-ksztalt-tekstu.png)

| Shape | What it does and what you set |
|---|---|
| **Arc** | Text along an arc. **Bend** in %: positive – arc up, negative – down. |
| **Circle**, **Ellipse** | Text all the way around, e.g. a seal: radius, start, letters facing out or in. |
| **Along a path** | Text along a line of any shape on the page (drawn with the pen or from the Library) — choose it in **Line from a shape on the page**. |
| **Warp** | Letters warped like an arc, bulge, wave, flag or taper; **Bend strength** in %. |

- With an arc and warps, the ◆ handle under the text bends it more or less.
- Double-click the text to edit it — while you type, it's shown straight.
- Text on a shape is a single line; font, color, styles and `{…}` fields work as usual.
- **Straight** brings back ordinary text.

## 5.15 Find and replace

The magnifier on the top bar or [⋯] → [Find and replace…] (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd>; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>H</kbd> —
opens straight with the replace field).

![Find and replace next to the page](k-znajdz.png)

1. Type the text to find. The list shows each match: the page or master and a bit of the surrounding text.
2. Click a match (or <kbd>Enter</kbd> in the field, <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> — backwards) — the editor
   opens that page and selects the found text.
3. To replace, type the new text in the second field and click [Replace] (the selected match)
   or [Replace all].

| Option | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Match case** | “Calendar” won't find “calendar”. |
| **Whole words** | “cat” won't find “cats”. |
| **Also in data** | Also searches the cells of data tables (→ 14). |
| **In style names** | Also searches the names of paragraph and character styles (→ 5.4). |

- Search covers all pages and masters, including texts in groups.
- Fields in braces, e.g. `{miesiac}`, stay untouched — unless you type a brace in the search text.
- The look of parts of the text (e.g. a bold word) stays in place after replacing.
- You can undo [Replace all] with a single [Undo].

## 5.16 Spell check

Words not in the Polish dictionary are underlined with a wavy red line — only in the editor, never in the PDF.

![Suggestions for an underlined word](k-pisownia-dymek.png)


1. Double-click the text and click (on a phone, tap) the underlined word — or right-click
   it. A bubble with suggestions and [Add to dictionary] appears next to the word.
2. Click a suggestion (e.g. „wcześniej”) — the word changes in that spot.
3. Add a proper name the dictionary doesn't know (e.g. a company name) with the [Add to dictionary] button.
   Your dictionary is stored in your account, so it works in every project.

All suspicious words of the selected text are also listed in the panel, in the **Spelling** part — there a suggestion
changes the word in all places in that text.

![The “Spelling” part in the text panel](k-pisownia.png)

- `{…}` fields, numbers, words with digits, web addresses and emails are skipped.
- You can turn spell checking off in the view menu: **Check spelling**.

## 5.17 AI text: fixing and translating

AI can fix, shorten or translate a text – in every project. Google Gemini writes it with your key (how to get a key –
→ 7.11); one change costs a fraction of a grosz.

1. Select a text frame – you change the whole text. Or double-click into the text and select a part – you change only
   that part.
2. Click [AI ▾] on the insert bar → **Improve or translate text…**.
3. Click what to do: [Shorten], [Expand], [Simpler], [More formal], [Fix mistakes], [To Polish], [To English] or
   [To Ukrainian].
4. After a moment the result is in the **Result (you can edit it)** field. Edit it if you like, or click another
   button to try something else.
5. [Replace] puts the result in place of the text. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> undoes.

Fields in braces, e.g. `{name}`, stay unchanged.

**Translating a whole project.** For many texts at once (e.g. a calendar in three languages) keep the texts in a data
table and translate the column: the **✦** button next to the column name in the **Data** window (→ 14.6), e.g.
“translate the opis column into English”. Other changes in many places at once can be done by the AI Assistant (→ 25.6).

## 6.1 Rectangle, ellipse and polygon

[Shapes ▾] → **Rectangle**, **Ellipse** and **Polygon or star** insert shapes with a fill and a stroke (→ 6.5, 6.6).

## 6.4 Pen and point editing

[Shapes ▾] → **Pen** (<kbd>N</kbd>) creates a shape from points:

- a click places a corner point, a click and drag — a point with a curve,

**Point editing:** double-click the shape or choose [Edit points]. Drag the points,
and use the handles of the selected point to change the curves. Double-clicking the line adds a point,

## 7.6 SVG drawings

An SVG file is inserted as an image: it looks faithful and stays sharp at any zoom, but you can't
change its colors or text.

[Break drawing apart into elements] (in the image properties) turns it on the page into a **group of ordinary
shapes and text** in the same place. You can change the shapes' color and points, and edit the text
like any other text. The SVG file doesn't change and stays in the project images window.

Nothing gets lost along the way:

- **clipping** and **color gradients** remain shapes,
- a **simple mask** (a white shape) becomes clipping, and a **pattern** (e.g. dots, check) — repeated
  shapes clipped to their figure,
- parts with **effects the editor doesn't have** (blur, filter shadow, gradient mask, color
  blending, line arrowheads, text on a path) remain an **image** in the same place, at print resolution.
  After breaking apart, the app tells you how many there are; the rest of the drawing is editable.

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> restores the whole drawing.

## 7.10 Border and soft edges

The photo's **Border** section: color (the **none** field turns the frame off), **Weight** in mm, **Stroke opacity**,

The **Soft edges** section (photos and shapes): **Transition width** in mm — how far from the edge the photo
smoothly fades into transparency; 0 means a sharp edge. **Type**:

| Type | Look |

## 10.5 Spreads

In a section with spreads, pages form pairs: left and right, like an open book.

- You edit the left and right page **together**. New elements go onto the current page — you choose it
  by clicking in its area.
- Margins are **mirrored**: inner by the spine, outer by the edge.
- An element can lie **across the spine**, e.g. a photo over the whole spread — each page prints
  its own part. An element belongs to the page its center lies on.
- The thick dark line is the spine. Don't put text right next to it — after binding it may not be visible;
  the app warns about elements that stick into the strip by the spine.
- The button with two sheets next to the zoom switches between the whole spread and a single page (on a phone
  a single page by default).
- **A page moves from right to left on its own** (and the other way round): when you add, delete or move a page
  before it (or switch **Start section on a left page**), its elements go to mirrored positions — a number
  by the outer edge stays by the outer edge (aligned to it), a photo by the spine stays by the
  spine, and a text frame fitted to the margins lands in the new margins. Text and images are not flipped.
  Mark an element that should stay where it is in the panel (**Other** → **Fixed position when the page changes side**);
  an element across the spine belongs to the pair of pages and does not move. Pages repeated from data too: a copy that
  falls on the other side has a mirrored layout.

## 10.6 Cover with a spine

A cover is a separate section with one wide page: back, spine and front. You create it in the section window
(**Section type** → **Cover**), in the section menu ([Convert to cover with spine]) or right away
in the new project window (the **Cover** field).

| Setting | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Binding** | **Softcover, perfect bound (with spine)**, **Saddle-stitched – stapled (no spine)** or **Hardcover (board with turn-in)** — for hardcover you set **Wrap**, **Crease**, **Board allowance**, **Board thickness** (mm) and **Spine**: **Rounded** (sewn block with a convex spine – the 3D mockup shows the curve) or **Straight** (flat). |
| **Body paper** | **Paper weight** — **Paper weight (g/m²)** and **Paper type** (uncoated, matte coated, gloss coated, bulky) — or **Sheet thickness** in mm from the printer. |
| **Body pages** | **Calculate automatically** — from all interior pages, including series repeats — or **Enter** the number. **Body is the sections** chooses which sections count when there are several. |

The diagram in the window shows the back, spine and front, with the result below, e.g. “Spine 6.6 mm · Cover 352.6 × 246 mm”. The cover width and height are calculated automatically.

**Dimensions** — when the printer gives you the spine or the whole cover size:

| Choice | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Calculate automatically** | Spine from the paper and page count, size from the interior format. |
| **Enter spine** | **Spine (mm)** from the printer instead of the calculated one. The rest is calculated as usual. |
| **Enter size** | **Cover width** and **Cover height** (mm, without bleed) — the back and front split the rest of the width equally around the spine. You can also enter the spine; an empty field — the spine is calculated. |


![Cover dimensions entered manually](k-okladka-wymiary.png)

![Section window: cover with spine, diagram and result](k-okladka-okno.png)

On the canvas, dashed lines show the folds (spine, creases, turn-ins), and blue frames — the safe areas of the back, spine and front.

![Cover on the canvas: back, spine, front](okladka.png)

When the interior gains pages or you change the paper, the cover recalculates itself: the back stays, the front moves together with the spine, text on the spine stays centered on it, and a background across the whole cover stretches. In the element panel, in the **Other** part, the **Attached to the cover by** list (automatically, back, spine or front) tells what the element sticks to; the **Fit width to spine** switch stretches a strip on the spine to its full width. The warning “Wider than the spine” means the element overlaps the back or front.

![Element pinned to a part of the cover](k-okladka-przypiecie.png)

> Note: masters assigned to the cover section don't move on their own.

**Cover material and corners.** In the same window:

| Field | What you choose |
|---|---|
| **Cover material** | **Printed cover** (printed board) or an unprinted covering: **Bookbinding cloth**, **Faux leather**, **Textured paper**, **Velvet (velour)**. |
| **Laminate** | For a printed cover: **matte**, **gloss**, **soft-touch** or **no laminate**. |
| **Cover cloth color** | Typical colors of the given covering (e.g. navy, burgundy, bottle green cloth) or **Other color…**. |
| **Corners** | **square** or **rounded** 3, 5, 6 or 10 mm — on the canvas, red arcs in the corners show what will be cut off. |

On a covering, the cover artwork is not printed — only elements with gold foil or embossing remain (Print colors →
Finishing). The 3D mockup shows the covering in the chosen color, with a cloth, leather or paper texture,
and the foil and embossing on it. **Preflight check** reminds you about the covering and corners.

**Print specification** (the **⋯** menu) downloads two files: text for an order or email, and JSON. They contain
the formats and page counts of the sections, binding and spine, body paper, cover material, laminate and corners, ribbon, signatures,
substrate, color mode, spot colors and finishes.

## 10.7 Signatures

An interior sewn or glued from signatures must have a page count divisible by the signature size. In the section settings choose **Signatures**: every 4, 8, 16 or 32 pages.

In the page list, under the section name, you'll see e.g. “46 of 48 – 2 missing for a full signature (×16)”. [Fill with blanks] adds the missing blank pages at the end of the section — you undo it like any change. **Preflight check** also reminds you of an incomplete signature, and for saddle-stitched binding — of a page count not divisible by 4.

![Signature counter and “Fill with blanks”](k-skladki.png)

## 10.8 Page numbering

You insert the page number with the `{page}` field — best in a master, so it's on every page (→ 11.7).
Numbering depends on the section settings (→ 10.4) and counts pages after series repeats. Only
book block sections are numbered (type **Regular**, **Block**, **Yearly calendar**): the cover, endpapers, dust jacket, insert, backing board
and separate prints have no numbers and don't shift the numbering — the first page of the block is number 1 even
when a cover and endpaper come before it. Other ways of writing
the number (Roman, with leading zeros) are in the field table (→ 15.2).

## 10.9 Changing the format with scaling

A calendar from A4 to A3, a book from B5 to A5 — you change the format together with the elements:

- one section: section window (→ 10.4) → [Change format and fit elements…],
- whole project: [⋯] → [Change project format…].

1. Choose a **New format** from the list or enter **Width (mm)** and **Height (mm)**; [Swap orientation] rotates the sheet.
2. Choose **What happens to elements**:

| Method | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Scale everything** | Elements, type, lines, margins, guides and masters change proportionally. **Scale**: **Fit the whole page (centered)**, **Fit to width** or **Fit to height**. |
| **Keep sizes, move relative to edges** | Sizes stay; an element sticks to the nearest edge or the center. Backgrounds and full-width or full-height strips stretch to the new page. |
| **Change format only** | Elements stay where they are. |

3. The **Now → After change** preview shows the first page before and after. Below it are notes, e.g. how many elements will end up outside the page.
4. [Change format]. You undo the whole change with a single [Undo] (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>).

![Format change with before and after preview](k-format.png)

## 10.11 Special sections: dust jacket, flyer, calendars, die lines, imposition

A special section works like a cover with a spine (→ 10.6): it knows what the printed thing is. It calculates its own
format, labels zones on the page (panels, flaps), draws fold lines (creases, dashed), perforation lines
(dotted) and cut lines (solid pink line), warns about text on a fold and elements beyond the cut line,
and in the PDF gives a sheet in full size. You choose it as the **Section type** with [Section] or in the section
settings (→ 10.4).
The diagram in the window shows zones and folds live, with the sheet dimensions below.

![Folded flyer in the section settings](k-sekcja-ulotka.png)

| Type | What it calculates |
|---|---|
| **Front / back endpaper** | Hardcover: a sheet two book-block pages wide, with a fold in the middle. The **On the cover (glued)** half is glued to the inside of the cover, the other is the first (front) or last (back) loose leaf of the book. **Printing**: blank, printed visible side or both sides; **Paper** (g/m²) — the loose leaf adds to the spine thickness. An endpaper has no page numbers. |
| **Dust jacket with flaps** | Back, spine and front from the project's hard cover (boards and spine), two flaps (**Flap**, 0 – about half the front) and an allowance for the fold at the board. If pages are added, the jacket recalculates itself and the front elements move with it. |
| **Folded flyer** | A sheet for 2, 3 or 4 panels, **Fold**: **Roll fold** (C-fold – the panel folded inward is narrower by the **Reduction** so it doesn't buckle) or **Accordion** (Z – equal panels). The section has two pages: outside (with the **Cover** panel) and inside. |
| **Calendar with dates (year)** | **Year**, **One page per** month, week or day, first day of the week and **Holidays** (Poland: fixed and movable – Easter, Pentecost, Corpus Christi). Creates a data table and a series: the first page of the section is repeated for each month, week or day. Changing the year recalculates everything. |
| **Three-month calendar** | A header, three calendar pads one below the other, a window on the middle one and an advertising strip – with a crease under the header and perforations. |
| **Die line** | **Envelope** (C6, DL, C5, square), **Presentation folder** with a pocket and business card slits, or **Box** (length, width, height → a net with a lid, bottom, flaps and a glue tab). **Printing on both sides** adds a second page – the inside of the sheet: zones, folds and the cut line are mirrored horizontally on it, as they'll fall when the sheet is turned over, so you design the inside of a box or envelope in the right place. Die lines go to the PDF only from the first page; the 3D mockup shows the inside. |
| **Imposition sheet** | Stickers, business cards, labels: you design one item (or a series of them from data), and in the PDF they stand side by side on a sheet (SRA3, A3, A4, B2) with crop marks. **Cutting**: rectangle (guillotine), **sticker** with rounded corners, **round / oval sticker** (a circle or oval within the item's size) or **tag with a hole** – clipped top corners (the clip in the rounding field) and a Ø 5 mm hole for string; the preflight check warns about elements in the hole and outside the die cut. Ready-made template: **Gift tags** (to whom, wishes, from whom – from a table). |

![Box die line on the page: zones, folds and cut line](k-sekcja-wykrojnik.png)

**Calendar-with-dates fields** to insert into text (→ 15): `{data}`, `{dzien}`, `{miesiac}`, `{dzien_tygodnia}`,
`{tydzien}`, `{swieto}`; the `wolne` column has the value “tak” on Sundays and holidays – use it in a data-driven
color (→ 17) to make holidays red. Your own columns added to this table stay after the year changes.

**In the PDF** (→ 21): the die cut line is a separate spot color **CutContour**, folds –
**Crease**, perforations – **Perforation**: the printer doesn't print them, only cuts, creases and perforates. For
the dust jacket, flyer and three-month calendar (cut straight), folds and perforations are marked with ticks outside
the format, next to the crop marks. An imposition sheet is laid out automatically when the export has **Sheet layout: Regular**.
Notes about text on a fold and elements beyond the cut line are also shown by **Preflight check** (→ 20).

The ready-made sections (→ 10.10) **Folded brochure**, **Business cards** and **Labels and stickers** are inserted right away
as a folded flyer and an imposition sheet, and a hardcover **Cover** — together with two blank endpapers.

**A hardcover book** consists of sections of these types: **Cover** (hardcover binding), **Front endpaper**,
**Block**, optionally an **Insert** (e.g. photos on coated paper — with its own paper, without page numbers) and **Back
endpaper**. The cover spine is calculated from the block and adds the loose leaves of the endpapers and the leaves of the inserts, each with its own paper.
**Preflight check** (→ 20) warns when a hard cover has no endpapers (the [Add endpapers] button adds
both, blank), and when an endpaper is used with a softcover. A hard cover can also have a **Bookmark – ribbon
in the spine** in a chosen color. In the PDF export (→ 21), **Files for the printer: cover, endpapers, book block…** gives
a separate file for each part: the cover, dust jacket, endpapers, block, inserts and each separate print.

A section from an older project that has “wyklejka” (endpaper) in its name but a regular type gets a one-time hint
“Section … looks like an endpaper – set the type…?” with a [Set type] button. It's only a suggestion — without a click
the section stays regular.

## 10.12 Paper models and cut-outs

A model made of paper or card from many parts – a little house, a box with dividers, a solid, a toy – you make in a section
of the **Paper model / cut-out** type. Each page of this section is one **sheet** with parts. In the section settings:

- **Sheet** – A4, A3, A5, Letter (home printer) or **Custom** (print shop sheet, plotter), portrait or landscape;
- **Material** – paper or card with a **paper weight** (thickness is calculated automatically: 300 g/m² card ≈ 0.39 mm), board
  or a custom thickness in millimeters; the width of slots depends on the thickness;
- **Printing and cutting** – **home printer** (cut and fold lines are printed – you cut with scissors and a craft knife)
  or **print shop with plotter** (the lines go to the PDF as separate colors: cut, folds and glue – the print shop doesn't
  print them); **printer margin** – the strip along the edge that the printer can't print on;
- **Back of the sheet** – unused, also printed, or **glue areas only** (unprinted).

The section margins equal the printer margin, and there is no bleed – the model is cut along the outline of the parts.

In this section the insert bar has extra tools:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| [Template] | A ready-made model from dimensions: **Box with a fitted lid** (two parts – the lid is larger by two material thicknesses and 0.5 mm of play, so it fits snugly but without forcing), **Box with a tuck-in flap** (a hinged lid with a tongue tucked behind the front wall – opens and closes without glue), **Box with a snap lock** (the lid's tongue goes into a slot in the front wall, as wide as the material thickness + 0.2 mm), **Rotating wheel** for books (two discs on a rivet – the top one with a window onto one of the bottom one's 3–12 fields; the window and the cross for the rivet are cut lines), **Slider** for books (a sheet with a slit near the edge and a T-shaped strip – the narrow part goes through the slit, the wide end stays under the sheet and keeps the strip from being pulled out; parameters: strip width and travel), **Pillow box** (two walls with arcs at the ends: a convex cut arc and a concave **curved fold** – once the ends are pushed in, the box closes itself; a curved fold prints as a crease along the curve, and the 3D mockup shows it flat because card bends springily along a curve), **Flaps** for books (lift-the-flap: 1–6 windows cut on three sides and folded on the fourth – the fold works as a hinge; a second sheet underneath for hidden pictures, glue only outside the windows), **Tray**, **Cuboid**, **Prism** (3–12 sides), **Pyramid**, **Cross-slotted dividers** (shorter by two thicknesses, lower by one, with slots of thickness + 0.2 mm), solids with curved walls – **Cylinder**, **Cone**, **Truncated cone** (cup, lampshade) and **Sphere from gores** (globe, 4–24 gores – each glued with tabs to the next), **Vase** (a solid of revolution from a profile, 6–32 segments – wall strips and a flat bottom), ready-made models **House with a gable roof**, **Car** and **Turtle** (a shell from segments, head, legs and tail – glued under the shell) (solids from a silhouette unfolded automatically, as in [3D model]): the lateral surface unrolled exactly (the sector angle calculated from height and diameters), bottom and lid from many short sides, tabs along the arc with one number (a seam – the check verifies that both sides have the same length) – **Pop-up card with a V-fold** (a triangle glued to two slanted fields at the same angle to the spine as its edges – it lies flat on the open card, stands up when closing and hides inside; angle 15–75°), **Pop-up card with a platform** (a card and a strip glued with tabs to fields on both sides of the spine: when opened it stands like a box, when closed it lies flat inside; **Layers: 2** glues a second, smaller platform on top of it) and **Pop-up card (cut-out)**: a single sheet with slits and folds from which boxes pop up when the card is opened at a right angle (1–4, depth and height to choose; the slits end before the edge, so nothing sticks out when closed); the **3D mockup** shows it open at a right angle with the boxes popped up). Parts have folds, tabs and pair numbers; the window states the section's material thickness. |
| [Part] | A new part – a 60 × 40 mm rectangle with a letter (A, B, C…). |
| [From shape] | Turns a selected rectangle, polygon, ellipse or pen shape (→ 6) into a part – any outline. Pen curves stay curves (densely, about every 2 mm), so round shapes cut smoothly. |
| [Solid from shape] | The selected shape is the base of a solid: a **prism** (walls perpendicular at a chosen height, lid at the first wall), a **pyramid** (walls up to an apex above the center of the base), or **walls with a roof** – two joined solids: walls with a bottom and a separate roof (a pyramid without a base, fanned out around the peak, with a **Roof height**); the tabs at the top of the walls have the same pair numbers as the bottom edges of the roof, so the roof is glued onto the walls like on a house. One part with a net is created – a wall on each side, tabs with pair numbers and folds. It works with any polygon, including a concave one (star, letter L) – with a concave one, check that the walls on the net don't overlap. |
| [3D model] | A solid from a 3D program or the web (**OBJ** or **STL**) unfolded flat – as in paper-model programs: Makietka splits the faces into triangles, unfolds them along the flattest edges (flat faces stay whole, without lines), adds mountain and valley folds with an angle, and tabs with pair numbers. When a part would overlap itself, further faces go into the next part. You give the model's largest dimension in mm; then [Lay out] arranges the parts on sheets. Models of up to a few hundred faces work best (in Blender: Decimate). You can add an **MTL** file to an OBJ (select both at once) – each part gets the color of the material that takes up most of it; without MTL, materials get colors from the palette. When the model has a **texture** (UV coordinates in the OBJ and a `map_Kd` image in the MTL), select the image file (PNG, JPG or WebP) together with them – each part gets a print: the image transferred onto its faces exactly as it lies on the model. The print goes to **Project images** (“… – nadruk.png”) and moves together with the part on [Lay out]; after [Split] and [Merge] it disappears (it covered the whole part). When the image is missing, Makietka tells you which file it is looking for. |
| [Fold] | Two clicks on the part's edges: a fold line divides it into faces. A line along the edge itself or outside the part isn't added. |
| [Join] | Click near the edge that will get a **glue tab**, then near the edge of another part (or the same one) it is glued to – both get **the same number**. A second click in the middle of a face instead of near an edge adds a **glue area** the length of the edge there. When the edges have different lengths, the app tells you. |
| [Glue] | A click in a part adds a glue area (with <kbd>Shift</kbd> – on the underside, unprinted, when the back of the sheet is for glue). |
| [Glue line] | Gluing isn't always at an edge: a helper line **anywhere on a face** – it doesn't have to reach an edge or be closed. With nothing selected: two clicks in the part (start and end). Draw a curve, wave or polyline on the part with a line, arc or the pen, select it and use [Glue line] – it turns into a glue line (a closed shape, e.g. a circle, too). Then [Join]: an edge or tab of another part, then the line – they get the same number. A **straight** line: the attached part stands at an angle (it folds on the line); a **curved** one: it bends and wraps along it. The line prints as a green stroke with a number, and in the file for the printer it goes to the glue plate; in the part panel (**Glue lines**) you see its length, change its number or move it to the underside. **Preflight check** compares the length of the edge and the line, and the assembly instructions say whether the attached part stands at an angle or wraps along a curve. |
| [Slit] | A cut line inside a part or from its edge, **at any angle** – two clicks (the ends snap to vertices, fold ends and other slits, up to 1.5 mm). Curves and polylines: draw a line, arc or pen shape on the part, select it and use [Slit] – one slit made of many segments is created. Slits together with folds divide a face: e.g. two slanted slits from the ends of a fold give a triangular flap that tilts on its fold in the 3D mockup (pop-up cut-out). A slit is printed on the cut plate together with the outline; in the part panel (**Slits**) you see its length and can delete it. **Preflight check** warns when a slit goes outside the part. A slot ([Slot]) is something else – a narrow opening with a width for a tab. |
| [Cutout] | A hole in a part: draw a closed shape on it (rectangle, ellipse, polygon, pen shape), select it and use [Cutout]. The piece inside falls out – in the editor it has grey hatching (it's waste), in the 3D mockup there's a hole in the face. **A window with a door**: instead of a cutout, make a [Slit] on three sides and fold the fourth side ([Fold] or slits from the ends of a fold) – the door swings on its hinge and reveals the opening. |
| [Onto part] | A face isn't just a color: select a text, image, shape or ornament lying on a part and use [Onto part] – the element goes onto the face and from then on is part of the part: it moves and rotates with it, stays in place when you change folds and tabs, travels with it on [Lay out] (elements lying wholly on a part are placed on it by [Lay out] itself), on [Split] goes to the piece it lies on, and in the 3D mockup sits on its face and folds with it. Face artwork is **clipped to the part's outline** – whatever sticks out doesn't print beyond the cut line or on the tabs. The part panel → **On faces** shows how many elements are on the faces; **Take out of part (to edit)** returns them to the page in the same place – e.g. to correct a text – then [Onto part] again. |
| [Lay out] | All the section's parts are arranged anew on the sheets – in rows within the print area (without the printer margin), tallest first; a part that doesn't fit horizontally is rotated 90°; missing sheets are added at the end of the section (with double-sided printing, parts go on the first page of the sheet). Other page elements stay. A part too big even after rotating stays in place – the app tells you. |
| [Instructions] | Assembly instructions from the model: what to print (material, paper weight), which parts to cut out (with sheet numbers), how many mountain and valley folds each part has, then gluing the pairs in order – “Put glue on tab 3 (part A) and stick it under edge 4 of part B with number 3”, “Slide tab 7 into slot 7”. [Download (.txt)] or [Insert as page] – a new A4 section after the sheets with numbered steps (editable like ordinary text). |
| [Slot] | Two clicks in a part: a slit for a tab or divider – the width is the material thickness + 0.2 mm of play. |
| Split (in the part panel) | Two clicks on the edges divide the part in two – e.g. when after [3D model] a part doesn't fit on the sheet. Edges keep their tabs and numbers, folds go to their part (those crossing the line are trimmed to it), and a new pair is created on the cut line: a tab on one part, an edge to glue on the other. |
| Merge (in the part panel) | A click near an edge with a pair number joins both parts into one: the second rotates and moves so the pair's edges coincide, and the glue edge becomes a fold (mountain, 90°). When the parts would overlap, Makietka refuses – they stay separate. |

<kbd>Esc</kbd> ends the tool. You move and rotate a part like any element; you can't stretch it
(its dimensions are the model's dimensions). A selected part has in the **Properties** panel:

- **Part** – its name (letter), face color, the **Pair numbers and part name** switch and tool buttons;
- **Edges** – each edge with its length and kind: **Cutting**, **Glue tab** (with width),
  **To be glued (no tab)** or **Tab into slot** (with length) – and a **pair number**;
- **Folds** – **mountain** (away from you, dash-dot – the print stays on the outside; template folds and new
  folds are like this) or **valley** (towards you, dashes) and **angle when folded** – the angle between faces in the finished model
  (box 90°, walls of a hexagonal prism 120°), and in a pop-up card the **Card spine (pop-up)** switch;
- **Glue areas** and **Slots** – pair number, dimensions, on the underside, deleting.

On the sheet, tabs are light grey with a pair number, and the same number stands by the edge or at the spot they are
glued to – after cutting out you know what to glue to what. The plotter file (menu **⋯**) and the PDF for the printer get
the cut outline of the parts with tabs and slots, folds (also at the base of each tab) and glue areas.

**What joins what.** When nothing is selected, the page panel in a paper-model section has a **Connections** list:
each number with what carries it, e.g. “1 – A: tab 2 ↔ B: edge 4”, and with several sheets also the sheet number.
Hovering over an item highlights both sides of the pair on the sheet in orange, clicking selects the part. A number
without a pair or used more than twice has a warning bar. Hovering the mouse over a part highlights all its pairs.

**Preflight check** (→ 20) watches over the model: a part sticking out of the sheet or into the printer
margin, parts overlapping each other including tabs (they can't be cut out), a number without a pair (you can't tell what to glue it to), a number used more than twice, a pair of edges
of different lengths, a glue area smaller than a tab, a slot narrower than the material or shorter than
the tab, and in a pop-up card – a part that sticks out of the card when it's closed (by how many millimeters), and a card that
doesn't close flat because a platform strip or V arm doesn't match the distance between glue areas. For each note, [Open template] selects the part.

The **3D mockup** (→ 19.4) has a **Paper model (from parts)** model for paper-model sections: first the sheets lie
flat, print side up, then each part folds along its folds – with the print from the sheet on its faces
and card on the inside (**Folded parts** – they stand side by side as they lie on the sheets). The last position,
**Glued model**, glues the parts in pairs into one solid – starting from the largest part, the next ones come to where they
should be glued: edge to edge (rotated so that all pairs of that part match; with no other pairs – at the
**Angle after gluing** from the edge panel: 180° flat, like an extension, 90° perpendicular), edge to a straight glue line, glue area or slot – the part stands
perpendicular on the print side, edge to a curved glue line – the part bends and wraps along the curve,
keeping its folds (e.g. a strip's visor stays folded).
Ornaments, texts and images on the faces travel with them. Parts with no pairs to the rest stay on the sheet.

**Pop-up in motion.** When a card's fold has **Card spine (pop-up)** turned on in the panel (pop-up templates have it
right away), an **Opening the pop-up card** slider (0–180°) appears in the mockup window. The card opens around the spine,
and the attached platforms, V-folds, second layers and cut-outs move as in a real book: faces are
rigid, folds act as hinges, and glue areas hold the attached parts. At 180° everything lies flat,
at 90° the elements stand up, and with the card almost closed they fold inward. The setting is saved in the shot.

**Angled elements from any part.** You don't need the V template: on a card with a spine, draw two straight
**glue lines** on both sides of the spine (slanted to it) and join the edges of any part to them – in the mockup
the part stands at the angle that follows from the geometry. The card panel shows the **angle to spine**
and the distance from it for each straight line, and the panel of the attached part has a **Pop-up** section: at what angle it stands with the card open
at 90°, how far it reaches from the card, and whether it hides inside the card when closed or sticks out (by how many millimeters).

## 10.13 Comic

A comic is an add-on to an ordinary publication: a booklet, a book, loose sheets or a file for viewing on screen.
Everything works without AI; AI (marked with a star ✦) can help write the script and draw the panels.

**How to start**

- **From a template:** **New project** → **Start from: Template** → **Comic**. You get pages with ready panels,
  a sample script, cards for two characters (Ala and Ola) and a place (Small town).
- **From a blank page:** **New project** → **Start from: Blank page** → tick **It is a comic – panels, bubbles, script and
  characters**.
- **In an existing project:** [Section] → type **Comic** (→ 10.4).

In the template you choose the comic's **Form**:

| Form | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Booklet with a cover – for print** | A booklet cover (with room for a title and a drawing) and the comic pages, saddle-stitched. |
| **Paperback book – for print** | A cover with a spine and the comic pages. |
| **Loose sheets – no cover** | Just the comic pages, e.g. for printing at home. |
| **On screen – PDF or presentation (landscape)** | Landscape pages like slides, without bleed – for viewing in the preview or as an on-screen PDF. |

![A comic page with panels and bubbles; Panels, Speech bubble and Comic on the bar](k-komiks.png)

**The comic bar.** On a comic page the insert bar has extra buttons:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| [Panels] | A ready panel layout for this page, the gap between panels, bleed; in the same window [Split panel]. |
| [Speech bubble ▾] | A menu: a **Speech**, **Thought**, **Shout**, **Whisper** or **Caption** bubble (→ 4.14), **Sound effect** (a big outlined “BOOM!” lettering) and **Bridge** (joins two selected bubbles of one character). |
| [Comic] | The **Comic step by step** window – the whole comic in five steps (described below). |

The comic AI tools – **Script with AI**, **Reference pictures of characters and places**, **Draw panels** – are in the
[AI ▾] menu (→ 7.11), and the chat with the AI – under [AI assistant] on the top bar (→ 25.6). When you have no Google
Gemini key, the drawing and writing items are greyed out, and at the top of the menu there is **You have no key for
generating pictures and texts – add a key…** (→ 7.11).

**Comic in the Library.** [Library] (→ 4.8) has a **Comic** category: bubbles of every kind and the sound effect, ready
panel layouts (a click replaces the panels on this page; pictures and bubbles stay), halftone and motion lines, and on a
comic page also **Comic windows**: **Comic step by step**, **Script**, **Characters, places and props**, **Upload ready
pages** and **Webtoon – for reading on a phone**. It is a good way on a phone, where the bar is shorter.

### Comic step by step

[Comic] opens a window with five steps. Each step shows what is already there (e.g. “Done: 5 lines on 2 pages.”).
Do them in order or go back to any of them – each button opens its own window.

![The Comic step by step window](k-komiks-okno.png)

1. **Story** – [Open the script] (the **Script** window, below) or [Write with AI…] (Script with AI, below).
2. **Characters, places and props** – [Characters, places and props…]: character, place and prop cards and the **Drawing style**.
   The thumbnails show the reference images.
3. **Panels and images** – [Panel layout…], [Draw this page's panels…] (AI) or [Upload finished pages…].
4. **Speech bubbles** – [Place bubbles]: texts from the script go into bubbles in the panels.
5. **Finished comic** – [Preview], [PDF…] (for print or for the screen) and [Webtoon] (a strip for reading on a phone).

### Step 1. Script

The **Script** window shows the comic page by page: panels in each page, lines in each panel. You don't have to type
numbers or remember column names.

![The Script window: page, panels, shot, place, who is in the panel and the lines](k-komiks-scenariusz.png)

In each panel:

| Field | What to choose or type |
|---|---|
| **Shot** | How the scene is seen: **wide shot** (the whole place), **full shot** (characters from head to toe), **medium shot** (from the waist up), **close-up** (the face), **detail** (an important object), **from above**, **from below**, **over the shoulder**. **Shot: any** – no requirements. |
| **Place** | A place from the cards (step 2) – its description and image become the panel's background. |
| **Characters in the panel** | Tick the characters visible in the panel, including those who say nothing (those who speak are ticked automatically). For each character in the panel choose an **emotion** (joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, curiosity, calm, tiredness) and type **what they are doing**, e.g. “running, waving”. The AI then draws the character with that expression and in that motion. |
| **Props in the panel** | Objects from the prop cards visible in the panel (e.g. **Parcel**). Their description and reference image go to the AI. |
| **What the panel shows** | Briefly, e.g. “Ala runs down the lane, houses behind her”. It does not go on the page – it is a description for the artist or the AI. |
| **Lines** | [+ Line]: **Who speaks** (a character from the cards or **Caption**), **Bubble kind** (speech, thought, shout, whisper) and **Bubble text**. The bin icon deletes the line. |

[+ Panel] adds a panel on the page, [+ Page] – a new script page; the bin icon next to a panel deletes the panel.
[Save] saves the script. You add characters and places in step 2 – then you pick them here from a list.

**For advanced users.** The script is an ordinary **Scenariusz** (script) data table (one row per line, columns
**strona** (page), **kadr** (panel), **ujęcie** (shot), **miejsce** (place), **w kadrze** (in the panel), **opis sceny**
(scene), **postać** (character), **tekst** (text), **rodzaj** (kind)). [Table] in the Script window opens it in the
**Data** window (→ 14) – there you can paste a script from a spreadsheet (→ 14.3) or add columns with other languages.
Page 1 is the first page of the comic (the cover does not count).

**Script with AI.** [Write with AI…] in step 1 (or [AI ▾] → **Script with AI**) writes the comic out for you – including the characters' emotions and actions:

1. Choose **From an idea** and describe the story in 3–5 sentences, e.g. “Two friends, Ala and Ola, find a parcel at
   their door. Inside there is a kitten…”, and set **How many pages**.
   Or choose **From a document** and a file with a script or story: Word (.docx), LibreOffice (.odt), Markdown (.md)
   or plain text (.txt). From Google Docs: **File → Download → Microsoft Word**. Dialogue from the document is kept
   word for word.
2. Click [Write it out]. After a few seconds you see a preview: pages, panels, who speaks, text, what the panel shows.
3. [Save script] – the script goes into the **Script** window (with shots, places and characters in the panels),
   character and place cards are created (existing cards are only completed), and pages without panels get panels.
   [Back] returns to the description without saving.

Cost – about 0.01 PLN; you see it before writing it out.

### Step 2. Characters, places and props

[Characters, places and props…] opens the cards. Describe a character once – the description and reference image make sure
it looks the same in every panel (for you, an artist or the AI).

![Character and place cards](k-komiks-postacie.png)

- **Character:** **Name**, **Appearance** (e.g. “8 years old, red braids, green dress”), **Distinctive feature** (e.g.
  “yellow backpack”), **Personality**, **Reference image** and **Bubble colour** (this character's bubbles get this
  background colour; white – a normal bubble).
- **Place:** **Place** (name), **Description**, **Time of day**, **Light** and **Reference image** – a view of the place
  itself, without characters. In panels with this place (chosen in the script) the place's image is the background,
  and the characters stand on it.
- **Reference image:** [Choose image…] – a drawing or photo from your device; or [Draw from description (AI)] – AI draws
  it from the card fields in the comic's style (you see the cost before drawing, about 0.14 PLN). A character – the
  whole figure on a white background; a place – a wide view without people.
- **Prop:** an object that comes back in many panels (a backpack, a kite, a map) – **Prop** (name), **Description**,
  **Whose (character)** and **Reference image** (the object alone on a white background). In the script you tick it in
  the panel (**Props in the panel**); a prop mentioned in the scene description is used by the AI even without ticking.
  The **Comic** template has a sample **Parcel**.
- **Drawing style** (at the top of the window; each tile shows the same sample scene in that style): **Colourful storybook**,
  **Watercolour**, **Simple cartoon**, **Manga (black and white)**, **Retro comic**, **Pencil sketch**, **European comic
  (clear line)**, **Superhero comic**, **Anime (colour)** or **Paper cut-out** – it applies to all AI images in this
  comic.

![Character card](k-komiks-postac.png)

Clicking a card opens it for editing, [Save] saves it, [Delete] removes the card (bubbles and images on the pages
stay). The cards are ordinary **Postacie** (characters) and **Miejsca** (places) tables – you also see them in the
**Data** window.

### Step 3. Panels and images

**Panel layout.** [Panels] on the bar (or [Panel layout…] in step 3) shows 9 ready layouts (1–9 panels). Click
a preview, set **Gap between panels (mm)** and, if you like, **Bleed** – panels at the edge then run off the page,
up to the trim line. [Lay out panels] replaces the panels on this page; images and bubbles stay.
[Split panel] in this window: click two points on the page – the line between them splits the panel in two (it can
be diagonal). <kbd>Esc</kbd> ends.

![The Panels window – 9 ready layouts](k-komiks-kadry.png)

A panel is a shape with a white fill and a black outline, numbered in reading order (rows from the top, left to right
within a row – in the layers “Panel 1”, “Panel 2”…). Move and change it like any shape; put an image into a panel
like into any shape (→ 7.7), e.g. by dragging a file onto the panel. A character can break out of the panel – then
place the image above the panel.

**Draw panels (AI).** Open a comic page and click [Draw this page's panels…] in step 3 (or [AI ▾] →
**Draw panels**). You don't write instructions for the AI – Makietka builds them from the script and the cards:

1. The window shows each panel of the page: a thumbnail, a field with the description and a tick box. The description
   is in plain words, e.g. “Panel 2. Shot: full shot. Scene: Ala runs down the street. In the panel: Ala – 8 years
   old, red braids…, always with: yellow backpack, emotion: joy, doing: running. Place (background): Small town…”.
2. Edit the description if you like. Panels with an image are unticked – tick them to draw them again.
3. Choose the **Quality** and check the cost below the list.
4. [Draw] – the panels fill one by one (“Drawing 2 of 3…”). The reference images of the characters and the place go
   to the AI together with the description, so the characters and the background look the same in every panel.
   There is no lettering in the images – the text is in the bubbles.

**The whole page as one picture.** In the drawing window tick **The whole page as one picture – cheaper and more
consistent; AI also draws the panel borders (the picture replaces the page's panels, bubbles stay)**. The AI gets the
descriptions of all panels at once, the page's panel layout and the characters' reference pictures, and draws the whole
page – with panel borders, one light and one line style. It costs as much as one picture (e.g. about 0.14 PLN in Draft
instead of about 0.42 PLN for three panels). The picture covers the whole page with bleed, and the page's panels
disappear (they are already in the picture). If you want to fix panels separately, draw panel by panel.

![A whole comic page drawn as one picture (Draft) – before adding bubbles](k-komiks-cala-strona.jpg)

**Draw again.** Don't like an image? Open [Draw this page's panels…] again and tick that panel. The description field
shows the description **the current image was made from** – change one word or draw again unchanged. [Description
from the script] goes back to a freshly built description (e.g. after changing the script or a card). Earlier images
stay in **Project images** (→ 7.5). A small change in one panel (e.g. “Ala should have her eyes closed”) needs no
redrawing: select the panel → [AI ▾] → **Fix the picture in the frame…** (→ 7.11).

**Pictures made elsewhere.** You don't have to draw in Makietka. Pictures from the free Gemini, from Flow, from another
generator, or your own drawings and scans go in like this:

1. Open the comic page where the pictures should start.
2. **Drag several files at once onto the page** (or a ZIP file) – or click [Upload ready pictures…] in step 3 and choose
   the files.
3. The pictures go one by one into empty panels: from this page on, in reading order, files in name order.
   Panels that already have a picture are skipped. If there are not enough panels, Makietka tells you which files it did not insert.

A single picture dropped onto a panel goes into that panel (→ 7.7). A number in the file name puts the picture in a specific place:

| File name | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| `01.png`, `page 7.jpg`, `page_3.png` | The whole page 1 (7, 3) with bleed. That page's panels disappear (they are already in the drawing); bubbles stay above the image. |
| `s03-k2.png`, `p3_panel2.jpg` | Panel 2 on page 3 – the image fills the panel (move it by double-clicking, → 7). |
| any other, e.g. `Gemini_Generated_Image_….png` | The next empty panel. |

Pages count from the first comic page; missing pages are added at the end of the section.

**Panel descriptions for free AI.** In the **Draw panels** window click [Copy panel descriptions]. Makietka copies to the clipboard
the descriptions of all panels on this page – with the drawing style and the request “no text and no speech bubbles”. Paste them into the free Gemini
or Flow, download the pictures and drag them onto the page (above). Without a Google Gemini key the window is available for exactly
this: [Draw] is greyed out, but copying the descriptions works.

### Step 4. Speech bubbles

[Place bubbles] puts bubbles with the script texts into the right panels, with the tail pointing down. Then:

1. Move the bubbles and tail ends to where they fit the drawing.
2. Fixing the script? Click [Place bubbles] again – only the text changes; bubbles you moved stay where they are.
   A line deleted from the script removes its bubble.

- When a character speaks twice in a row in the same panel, the second bubble goes below the first and is joined to
  it with a **bridge**. By hand: select two bubbles (with <kbd>Shift</kbd>) → [Speech bubble ▾] → **Bridge**; select a joined bubble and
  choose **Bridge** again – you unjoin them.
- You can also insert a bubble by hand: [Speech bubble ▾] → the bubble kind (→ 4.14). **Sound effect** is a big outlined
  “BOOM!” – change the text, enlarge, rotate.
- **Comic styles.** Bubbles use the **Dialogue** style (Comic Neue, capitals), effects the **Sound effect** style
  (Bangers, yellow with a black outline). Changing a style (→ 5.4) changes all bubbles or effects at once. In
  a Ukrainian project both styles use the Pangolin font (with Cyrillic).
- **Language versions.** Add columns `tekst_en`, `tekst_uk`… to the script table (the **Data** window). [Place bubbles]
  then asks which column to take the texts from – the same comic gets the texts in another language. AI can also
  translate a column: the **✦** button next to the column name (→ 14.6), e.g. “translate the tekst column into
  English”.

### Step 5. Finished comic

- [Preview] – the whole comic page by page or in spreads (→ 19).
- [PDF…] – a file for the print shop or for the screen (→ 21). Save a comic in the **On screen** form as an on-screen
  PDF – you open it on a computer or phone like a presentation.
- [Webtoon] – the comic pages one below another as images in a ZIP file, ready to upload to a platform for reading
  comics on a phone.

**Preflight check** (→ 20) watches the comic: bubble text smaller than 7 pt, a bubble going off the page or lying
across two panels, a tail pointing outside the panels, and empty panels (no image). For a bubble warning,
[Open template] selects it on the page.

**Halftone and speed lines.** In [Library] (→ 4.8), **Backgrounds and patterns** has comic patterns: **Comic
halftone**, **Speed lines (radial)** – for a blast or surprise – and **Speed lines (horizontal)** – for speed. You can
put the same pattern on a selected shape, e.g. a panel (→ 6.5).

# 11. Master pages

## 11.1 What a master is and when to use it

Some things repeat on many pages: **page number, header, footer, background, frame,
writing lines**. Instead of inserting them on each page separately, you insert them once — as a **master**.

- A master lies **under** the content of every page that has it. You can't select it on the page itself
  or move it — so it won't get shifted by accident.
- You change it once, and **all** those pages change.
- The `{page}` field in a master shows each page's own number: one master, and on the pages 1, 2, 3…
- A page can have several masters at once, e.g. “Grid” under the content and “Page number” at the bottom.

When a master, and when an ordinary element? If something should be **on many pages in the same place** —
a master. If only on one page — an ordinary element.

![Master pages window: what it is and ready-made masters](k-wzorce-galeria.png)

## 11.2 Where the masters are

- [Masters] under the page list (next to [Section]) and on the editor's top bar — opens the **Master pages** window.
- Page ⋯ menu → [Masters of this page…] — goes to that page and opens the window.
- Section ⋯ menu → [Section masters…].
- Page properties (nothing selected) → the **Masters** part: which masters are on this page;
  as long as the project has no masters, there's a [Choose a ready-made master…] button there.
- Library → the **Master pages** category — the same ready-made masters as in the window.

A page with a master on it has a small purple mark on its thumbnail; the tooltip on it says which masters they are.

## 11.3 Ready-made masters

Lower down in the **Master pages** window there is a gallery of ready-made masters with previews — in the diagram the master's elements
are distinct, and the faint blocks are page content. Groups:

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Numbering | number at the bottom, by the outer edge, “Page 5 of 120”, in a circle, on a side bar, Roman |
| Headers and footers | section name, project and section, colored band, footer with contact details |
| Backgrounds and paper | lines, grid, dots, graph paper, exercise book with margin, colored background, watermark |
| Frames | in the margins, double, rounded, page border, corners |
| Calendar and planner | tab on the edge, day header, “Notes” column, week |
| Book | running head with chapter title, number and chapter at the top |
| Catalog and offer, Newsletter, Invitation | logo and contact details, masthead, columns, decorative frame, bands |

Clicking a card adds the master to the project and **right away to all pages of the section** of the open page —
the window closes, and the master is visible on the page. [Undo] in the message or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>
reverses it in one step. Ready-made masters adapt to the section's format and margins, and take colors
and fonts from the brand kit (→ 27.9) if you have one. Once added, it's an ordinary master — you can change it.

## 11.4 Which pages a master is on

In the window, in the **In this project** part, next to each master:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| [+ Add to this page] | Turns the master on only on the open page. Clicking again (✓) removes it. |
| [+ To whole section] | Turns the master on for the section's pages that don't have their own choice. |
| [Edit] | Opens the master for editing (→ 11.5). |
| [⋯] | [Rename], [Duplicate], [Move up], [Move down], [Delete master]. |

You can also choose masters for a single page in the page properties: **Masters on this page** →
**As in section** or **Custom selection** and the switches next to the names.

![Masters window](okno-wzorcow.png)

## 11.5 Your own master and editing

[Draw your own master] creates an empty master and opens it right away. [Edit] opens an existing one.
The purple bar above the page reminds you that you're changing a master — everything you draw here will appear
on the pages with this master; [Done] returns to the pages. On ordinary pages, master elements are visible
in the layer list, and clicking there opens the master.

![Editing a master](wzorce.png)

## 11.6 Above content and mirroring

Master elements lie **under** the page content. Select an element in the master and turn on:

- **Above page content** — the element will cover the content, e.g. a frame or a watermark,
- **Mirror on left-hand pages** — an element by the right edge (e.g. a page number)
  moves to the left edge on the left pages of spreads. Ready-made masters “by the outer edge”
  have this turned on.

## 11.7 Fields in a master

Type a text with a field in the master, e.g. `Page {page} of {page_count}`, `{section}` or `{chapter}`
(the title of the current chapter). Each page will show its own value. The easiest way is to start from a ready-made
master with a page number (→ 11.3).

# 12. Guide lines, grid and zoom

Things that help you position elements and don't print: margins, bleed, grid, guides, zoom.

## 12.1 Lines on the page

Guide lines are not printed:

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Red** | The page edge after trimming. |
| **Pink band** | Bleed. Stretch backgrounds and photos that should reach the edge all the way to its end. |
| **Blue dashed** | Margin. Keep texts inside. |
| **Thick dark** | The spine of a spread. |
| **Dashed with labels** | On a cover: folds — spine, creases, turn-ins (→ 10.6). |
| **Turquoise** | Guide (→ 12.4). |

The guide lines button (<kbd>W</kbd>) next to the zoom hides them all.

## 12.2 Warnings

An element in the margin or beyond the bleed, text that doesn't fit, or a field with a typo get
an orange frame and go to the **Warnings** list. On a computer it's opened by the triangle button
next to the zoom (<kbd>F8</kbd>) — the number on it tells you how many notes there are; the window doesn't block your work, you close it with
the cross or <kbd>Esc</kbd>. On a phone — the [Warnings] button at the bottom of the screen. Clicking an item
selects the element. When it's intentional (e.g. a background into the bleed), click [Mute].

![Warnings](ostrzezenia.png)

## 12.3 Grid

In the view menu (→ 3.6): **Grid** shows lines every **Grid step** (in mm, from the page corner),
and **Snap to grid** puts the top left corner of the element you're moving on a grid line.

![Grid and guides](siatka.png)

## 12.4 Rulers and guides

- **Rulers** show millimeters; zero is in the corner of the open page.
- You pull a **guide** out of a ruler: from the top one downward — horizontal, from the left one to the right — vertical.
  A guide can be moved; dragged off the page, it disappears.
- In the page properties, the **Guides** part lets you enter a position as a number and add
  a vertical or horizontal guide.
- **Lock guides** in the view menu protects them from being moved by accident.

## 12.5 Snapping and distances

When you move or resize an element, it snaps to the edges, margins and center of the page,
to guides and to other elements:

- **pink line** — an edge or center lined up with another element,
- **arrows with a number** — the distance in mm to the nearest elements,
- **purple numbers** — a gap equals another gap in the row.

Holding <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> temporarily turns snapping off. Holding <kbd>Alt</kbd> with an element
selected also shows the distances to the page edges.

![Equal spacing](odleglosci.png)

## 12.6 Zoom

- [−] and [+] in the bottom right corner; clicking the percentage fits the page to the window (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>0</kbd>).
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + mouse wheel zooms in at the cursor.
- Space + dragging moves the view.
- On a touch screen — two fingers (→ 28.1).

# 13. Book: table of contents, running head, footnotes, cross-references, index

Table of contents, chapter title in the footer, footnotes, cross-references and index — they update themselves.

## 13.1 In brief

Page numbers come from the section numbering (→ 10.8), including pages repeated by a series (→ 16).

![Table of contents, footnote and running head on book pages](ksiazka.png)

## 13.2 Headings

Headings are texts in paragraph styles (→ 5.4) that the project treats as chapter titles. The table of contents, running head and cross-reference list are built from them.

- Without any settings, the **Title** style is heading 1 (chapter), and **Heading** – heading 2.
- You set other styles in the table of contents panel: for each style choose **heading 1**, **heading 2**, **heading 3** or **not a heading**.

> Tip: give all chapter titles the same style – then the table of contents and running head arrange themselves.

## 13.3 Table of contents

1. Open [Library] → **Book** → **Contents** (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> and “table of contents”).
2. The table of contents lands on the page as text and immediately fills with headings and page numbers.
3. You change its look (font, size, line spacing) as in any text.

![Table of contents and its panel](k-spis-tresci.png)

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | **chapters only**, **chapters and subchapters** or **three levels**; lower levels are indented. |
| **Dot leaders to page number** | Fills the space between the title and the number with dots. |
| **Style “…”** | Which style is a heading and at what level (→ 13.2). |
| [Refresh now] | Builds the table of contents right away. Usually not needed – it refreshes itself after every change. |

Flow a long table of contents into another frame, also on the next page (→ 5.7). Don't type the table of contents by hand – at the next refresh it would be rebuilt from scratch.

## 13.4 Running head

A running head is the title of the current chapter in the page footer or header.

1. Open the footer master (→ 11).
2. In the footer text choose **Insert field** → **{chapter}**. You can put the page number next to it: `{chapter} – {page}`.

On each page the field shows the last heading 1 up to that page. `{chapter:2}` shows heading 2 – a new chapter resets it.

## 13.5 Footnotes

1. Select a text (or type in it – the footnote will go at the cursor position).
2. In the panel, in the **Footnotes, cross-references, index** part, choose [Insert footnote] (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> and “footnote”).
3. Type the content in the **Footnote 1** field.

![Footnote in the panel and at the bottom of the page](k-przypisy.png)

- A superscript number appears in the text, and the content – at the bottom of the same page, above the bottom margin, under a short rule. In the editor you see it right away, but you can't select it: you change it in the panel.
- Numbers are counted automatically. **Footnote numbering**: **Restart in each section**, **Restart on each page** or **Across the whole project**.
- Text flowing through several frames takes the footnote with it: the footnote is on the page where its number stands.
- Footnote look: the paragraph style named **Footnote** (if there isn't one – 8 pt type).
- [Delete footnote] removes both the number from the text and the content.

## 13.6 Cross-references

A cross-reference is the page number of another place in the book, e.g. “see p. 12”.

1. Select the text where the cross-reference should go.
2. In the **Footnotes, cross-references, index** part, choose a heading from the **Insert cross-reference to** list.
3. “p. 12” appears in the text – the number updates itself when pages move.

In the editor a cross-reference shows as a `{s:…}` field; you'll see the number in the preview and in the PDF. If you delete the heading, a “?” appears instead of the number, and **Preflight check** reminds you of it (→ 20).

## 13.7 Index

1. Select a word in a text you are typing.
2. Choose [Add to index] (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> and “index”). With nothing selected, the app asks for the entry.
3. Insert the index: [Library] → **Book** → **Index**.

The index lists entries alphabetically (Polish order: ą after a, ł after l) with the numbers of all pages where the word appears in any text – letter case doesn't matter, and consecutive pages merge into a range (3–5). A text's entries are shown in the panel as chips; clicking a chip removes the entry.
